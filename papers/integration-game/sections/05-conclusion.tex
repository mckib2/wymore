\section{Conclusion} \label{sec:conclusion}

This paper introduced \emph{The Integration Game}, a systems engineering methodology for automated, semantically enabled systems integration inspired by A.~Wayne Wymore's Tricotyledon Theory of System Design (T3SD).  We demonstrated that enriching Wymorian buildability catalogs with a concept of Color $\kappa = (C, L, P, \rho, k, \pi)$, Route Carriers, and structured Mediation Operator families yields a typed coupling algebra in which legal Plays correspond to well-formed System Coupling Recipes over $TYR_{\mathcal{K}}$ (\textbf{RQ1}).  We proved the correspondence between Wymorian $IOR$s and uLSCs and established a Winning Condition that considers Color alignment, kinematic flow compatibility, and trajectory-level behavioral satisfaction with decidable finite-technology verification (\textbf{RQ2}).  We formulated an Integration Cost Functional with an explicit fitness map $\mathcal{S}$, compiled uLSC contracts into finite-state Reward Machines, and used a linear relational policy with clipped PPO-style updates to synthesize mediation architectures offline and regulate fault-tolerant routing online on compact synthetic catalogs (\textbf{RQ3}).  Empirical evaluation across a fifteen-scenario synthetic suite shows that progressive demos (S1--S15) exercise the three-tier Winning Condition, while relational masked synthesis on S2--S7 overcomes Tier~1--2 failures, partially transfers relational weights zero-shot to holdout S15, and maintains contract satisfaction under S12 link disruptions within the reported synthetic scope.
