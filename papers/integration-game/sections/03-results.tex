\section{Results} \label{sec:results}

To evaluate our proposed methodology across our three research questions, we developed a reference implementation in Python paired with the Z3 SMT solver~\cite{deMouraZ32008} as the symbolic backend.  The implementation realizes Color $\kappa$, the configurable Technology Catalog $TYR_{\mathcal{K}}$, synchronous discrete-time simulation of the Coupled Resultant $Z_b = \mathrm{RSY}(\mathcal{P})$, single- and multi-Consumer uLSC monitors compiled into Reward Machines, symbolic fragment verification, and the offline and online Markov Decision Process environments.

\subsection{Progressive Verification Suite (Scenarios S1--S15)} \label{sec:res-catalog}

We designed a fifteen-scenario verification suite (Scenarios S1--S15) that systematically isolates canonical systems-integration anomalies across Color axes and stateful behavioral contracts (with Scenario S15 reserved as an unseen industrial avionics holdout).  Each scenario begins with incompatible Producer Offers and Consumer Needs, exposes deceptive Tier~3 behavioral traps (e.g., lower-cost Mediation Operators that satisfy Tier~1 Color alignment and Tier~2 kinematic compatibility but violate trajectory-level obligations) and admits a verified winning Play:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Closed-Loop Autonomy (S1) \& Causal Ping-Pong Handshake (S2):}
        Evaluates autonomous feedback coupling and push-to-pull queue mediation in which a retain-on-read latch violates quiet-tick silence obligations, whereas a consume-on-read FIFO queue satisfies the uLSC Main-Chart.
  \item \textbf{Multi-Representation Gateway (S4) \& Hierarchical Entity Recoloring (S5):}
        Evaluates multi-hop runtime ABI marshaling ($\Delta\rho$; e.g., C-ABI $\leftrightarrow$ Python-object $\leftrightarrow$ DDS language binding) together with UDDL entity-composition projection and affine thermal conversion ($\Delta C, \Delta L, \Delta P$). Route Carriers handle bus/transport edges; uncalibrated identity casts that ignore ABI mismatch pass superficial typing but trigger Hot Breaches on the value envelope.
  \item \textbf{Dynamic State Estimation (S3), Non-Commutative Derivation (S6) \& Hard Deadline Synthesis (S7):}
        Evaluates multi-input computational derivations where naive finite-difference estimators, swapped-operand kinetic-energy derivations ($\frac{1}{2}v m^2$ versus $\frac{1}{2}m v^2$), or high-latency batch solvers satisfy static port typing but breach accuracy or deadline invariants.
  \item \textbf{Multi-Consumer Fan-Out (S8), Multi-Rate Clock Bridging (S9) \& Temporal Window Join (S11):}
        Evaluates composite multi-Consumer uLSC contracts, multi-rate mediation across stores, pumps, and freshness gates, and bounded-window correlation across asynchronous streams.
  \item \textbf{Provenance Triple-Modular Redundancy (S10), Redundant Link Failover (S12), Stateful Transaction FSM (S13) \& Pareto Trade Study (S14):}
        Evaluates provenance-weighted majority voting, runtime failover multiplexing between primary and backup Route Carriers, stateful accumulation with silence invariants prior to threshold fulfillment, and multi-objective Pareto trade-offs across competing winning architectures.
  \item \textbf{Avionics Multi-Bus Holdout (S15):}
        Models a FACE/SOSA-inspired avionics integration that couples navigation telemetry over a MIL-STD-1553 Route Carrier to an on-demand DDS guidance Consumer, requiring ABI marshaling $\Delta\rho$, coordinate-frame recoloring $\Delta L$, and push-to-pull mediation as an external generalization holdout.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Cost Axiom Verification, Sensitivity Analysis, and Symbolic CEGIS} \label{sec:res-cost}

To address \textbf{RQ1} and \textbf{RQ2}, we first audited the Integration Cost Functional $\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{P})$ and fitness transformation $\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{P})$ against Axioms (A1)--(A7) across Scenarios S1--S14 and a 16-point hyperparameter grid spanning operator costs, semantic distance weights, discount factors $\gamma$, and dynamic quality-of-service weights. Across all scenarios and weight perturbations, all seven axioms hold without exception. In particular, the lexicographic breach margin $\Omega$ guarantees strict separation between feasible and infeasible architectures (Axiom A1), yielding $\max \mathcal{J}_{\text{win}} = 6.30 < \min \mathcal{J}_{\text{lose}} = 10010.20$ and $\min \mathcal{S}_{\text{win}} = 93.70 > \max \mathcal{S}_{\text{lose}} = 92.58$. Within the feasible design subspace of Scenario S14, varying the precision-loss weight $w_{\text{loss}}$ and discount factor $\gamma$ smoothly shifts the Pareto ranking between low-latency narrowing pipelines and higher-cost full-precision pipelines without disturbing Tier~1--3 feasibility dominance.

Second, we evaluated the symbolic SMT verification gate across the decidable contract fragments ($\mathsf{QF\_LRA}$, $\mathsf{QF\_NRA}$, and $\mathsf{QF\_LIRA}$) for affine unit conversions, non-commutative polynomial derivations, and bounded-horizon queue, freshness, redundancy, and accumulation schedules. In Scenario S6 (Non-Commutative Derivation), when the initial verification suite $ITR_{\text{test}}$ contains only symmetric test trajectories ($m = v = 10$), a flawed Play that reverses the mass and velocity ports passes finite-trace evaluation via coincidence-point overfitting. Invoking the symbolic SMT gate immediately identifies a counterexample assignment ($m \neq v$), which the CEGIS loop appends to $ITR_{\text{test}}$, refuting the flawed Play and compelling synthesis to recover the true physical wiring.

\subsection{Offline Architecture Synthesis and Zero-Shot Generalization} \label{sec:res-offline-rl}

To address \textbf{RQ3} in the offline architecture synthesis regime, we evaluated five automated synthesis methods across the single-task causal handshake problem (Scenario S2, without distractor traps), the six-task synthesis benchmark (Scenarios S2--S7, augmented with lower-cost Tier~3 trap operators including retain-on-read latches, uncalibrated unit casts, naive finite-difference estimators, swapped-operand derivations, and high-latency batch solvers), and the unseen avionics holdout (Scenario S15):
\begin{enumerate}[topsep=2pt, itemsep=1pt, leftmargin=1.8em]
    \item \emph{Masked Random:} Uniformly samples legal Mediation Operators and compatible port connections.
    \item \emph{Greedy Lookahead:} Myopically selects moves that minimize static operator cost plus Tier~1--2 semantic and kinematic frontier potential $\Phi_{\mathcal{C}}$.
    \item \emph{Beam Search ($B=5$):} Expands a bounded frontier of partial Plays ranked by Tier~1--2 potential and static cost.
    \item \emph{Relational Softmax REINFORCE:} A policy-gradient agent parameterized over ontology-grounded relational compatibility features $\phi(s, a)$.
    \item \emph{Relational Masked PPO:} An actor--critic agent trained via Masked Proximal Policy Optimization~\cite{Schulman2017PPO, Huang2022Masking} conditioned on $\phi(s, a)$, intermediate Reward Machine progress pulses, and potential-based shaping.
\end{enumerate}

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:offline-rl}, when no behavioral trap operators are present in the catalog (Scenario S2, trap-free), Tier~1--2 greedy lookahead, beam search, Softmax REINFORCE, and Masked PPO all achieve a $100.0\%$ win rate with optimal residual cost $C_{\mathrm{res}} = 1.50$. On the full multi-task benchmark (Scenarios S2--S7) containing lower-cost Tier~3 traps, however, myopic Tier~1--2 greedy lookahead collapses to a $16.7\%$ win rate (and $0.0\%$ on the avionics holdout S15 in Table~\ref{tab:offline-holdout}). Because greedy lookahead evaluates only static semantic alignment, kinematic compatibility, and operator cost, it systematically prefers cheaper trap operators that assemble into syntactically legal Plays but trigger Hot Breaches during trajectory execution. By contrast, Relational Masked PPO leverages uLSC Reward Machine feedback and the lexicographic breach margin $\Omega$ to reject deceptive operators, achieving a $100.0\%$ win rate and matching the analytical reference optimum ($C_{\mathrm{res}} = 1.58$) across S2--S7. When transferred zero-shot to the unseen avionics holdout S15 (Table~\ref{tab:offline-holdout}), the learned relational policy immediately resolves the multi-hop representation, coordinate-frame, and mode-bridging chain ($50.0\%$ zero-shot win rate, breaking ties uniformly between the two unseen buffer variants) and converges to a $100.0\%$ win rate ($C_{\mathrm{res}} = 2.50$) upon brief fine-tuning.

\subsection{Online Adaptive Mediation Under Link Disruptions} \label{sec:res-online-rl}

To evaluate online supervisory control (\textbf{RQ3}), we examined Scenario S12 (Redundant Link Failover), in which a deployed Mediation Board routes navigation updates from either a low-cost primary line-of-sight link or a higher-cost transcoded satellite backup link under stochastic primary link dropout bursts ($4$--$6$ consecutive ticks per $16$-tick operational episode). Table~\ref{tab:online-rl} compares static primary routing, a reactive threshold heuristic (which switches to the backup link only after observing a dropped primary tick), and an online Actor--Critic controller conditioned on link health indicators and the uLSC Reward Machine state. Whereas static routing and reactive threshold switching incur Hot Breaches on every dropout burst ($0.0\%$ Tier~3 satisfaction), the online Actor--Critic controller preemptively engages the backup path during primary link degradation, achieving $100.0\%$ Tier~3 behavioral contract satisfaction with zero recovery delay while restricting backup channel utilization to $30.6\%$ of operational ticks.

Removing intermediate shaping pulses increases mean episodes-to-first-win sixteen-fold on both scenarios (from $1.0$ to $16.0$) while preserving wins.
