\section{Discussion} \label{sec:discussion}

A central implication of this work is that achieving rapid capability integration under a MOSA requires elevating interface specifications from static syntactic documents to executable semantic-behavioral contracts. Traditional Interface Control Documents (ICDs) and data-model alignments verify what we formalize as Tier~1 compatibility: ensuring that Fields are structurally present and mapped to commensurate Conceptual Entities, Logical Frames, and Platform Realizations. As our empirical evaluation demonstrates across Scenarios S2--S7 and S15 (Tables~\ref{tab:offline-rl} and~\ref{tab:offline-holdout}), however, Tier~1 semantic alignment and Tier~2 kinematic compatibility are necessary but fundamentally insufficient conditions for system integration. A mediation pipeline that matches every UDDL $(C, L, P)$ attribute and bridges push-to-pull transfer modes can nonetheless fail catastrophically at the trajectory level—for example, by retaining stale samples across quiet intervals, introducing phase lag that violates downstream control deadlines, or misordering non-commutative physical operands.

By unifying UDDL with Wymore's T3SD coupling algebra and uLSCs, \emph{The Integration Game} makes these cross-cutting integration failure modes structurally explicit. Rather than relying on manual authoring discipline or post-hoc integration testing to catch behavioral discrepancies, the Winning Condition embeds semantic typing, kinematic flow compatibility, and trajectory-level contract satisfaction into a single formal verification gate.

\subsection{Cost Structure and Learning-Guided Architecture Search} \label{sec:disc-cost}

In classical systems engineering trade studies, evaluating candidate architectures requires balancing hard feasibility constraints against multi-objective performance and cost figures of merit~\cite{Wymore1993}. When automated search or reinforcement learning agents optimize a composite scalar objective, poorly structured cost functionals invite reward hacking: an agent may leave Consumer Needs unconnected to avoid operator placement costs, instantiate disconnected interior blocks that artificially reduce naive semantic distance metrics, or accept minor behavioral contract breaches in exchange for lower static complexity.

The axioms developed in Section~\ref{sec:meth-cost} prevent these pathologies structurally. Grounding Tier~1 and Tier~2 frontier potentials strictly on the live offer frontier $\mathcal{F}_{\text{live}}$ (Axiom A2) ensures that interior Mediation Operators cannot reduce potential unless they are causally connected to upstream Producers. Route-wiring continuity (Axiom A4) and monotonic potential reduction (Axiom A3) provide a smooth, dense shaping landscape for multi-hop graph assembly, while the lexicographic breach margin $\Omega$ (Axiom A1) guarantees that no static or dynamic cost savings can compensate for a Tier~3 Hot Breach. As the ablation results in Table~\ref{tab:ablations} confirm, both the Markovian progress signals supplied by the uLSC Reward Machine and the lexicographic separation enforced by $\Omega$ are essential for sample-efficient convergence to valid mediation architectures. Moreover, parameterizing the policy over ontology-grounded relational compatibility features $\phi(s, a)$ enables zero-shot structural transfer to unseen component catalogs (Table~\ref{tab:offline-holdout}), demonstrating that the agent learns generalizable rules of semantic and kinematic mediation rather than memorizing scenario-specific wiring sequences.

\subsection{Decidability Boundaries and Assumption-Commitment Verification} \label{sec:disc-decidability}

A foundational question for automated systems integration is how to reconcile the tractability of finite-trace simulation with the rigor of universal behavioral verification across all admissible trajectories $f \in ITR$. Evaluating candidate Plays solely on a finite test suite $ITR_{\text{test}}$ provides sub-millisecond feedback suitable for inner-loop reinforcement learning (Table~\ref{tab:scale}), but remains vulnerable to coincidence-point overfitting whenever test trajectories fail to excite subtle algebraic or timing discrepancies. Conversely, full symbolic verification of arbitrary nonlinear cyber-physical dynamics over unbounded horizons is undecidable in general.

Our hybrid verification architecture resolves this tension by exploiting a key structural property of practical MOSA mediation: whereas the internal physics of Producer and Consumer subsystems may be arbitrarily complex, the interior Mediation Operators that bridge their interfaces—unit and coordinate conversions, bit-width casts, bounded FIFO queues, latest-value latches, freshness gates, and finite-state transaction monitors—possess bounded memory and fall within decidable quantifier-free SMT fragments ($\mathsf{QF\_LRA}$, $\mathsf{QF\_NRA}$, and $\mathsf{QF\_LIRA}$). Pairing fast concrete rollouts with a terminal symbolic CEGIS gate allows the synthesis loop to enjoy the speed of simulation-based RL while guaranteeing that any algebraic or scheduling coincidence on $ITR_{\text{test}}$ is automatically refuted via solver-generated counterexamples (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}).

\subsection{Threats to Validity and Future Directions} \label{sec:disc-limitations}

Several methodological boundaries define the scope of the present study and point toward natural extensions:
\begin{enumerate}[topsep=2pt, itemsep=1pt, leftmargin=1.8em]
    \item \textbf{Discrete Synchronous Time Base:} Following Wymore's discrete-system formulation, the Coupled Resultant $Z_b = \mathrm{RSY}(\mathcal{P})$ evolves over a synchronous global tick. Extending the game to continuous-time or asynchronous discrete-event specifications (e.g., via DEVS~\cite{ZeiglerPraehoferKim2000, wach2021devs} or timed automata) will allow finer-grained modeling of network jitter and clock drift across distributed buses.
    \item \textbf{Impulsive Flow Kinematics:} Within the Flow Kinematics tuple $\mathbf{k} = (x, v, a)$, flow acceleration $a$ serves in our current formulation as an architectural indicator capturing impulsive mode transitions across stores and pumps and rate-mismatch exposure across multi-rate boundaries. Developing continuous fluid-queue or network-calculus bounds driven directly by $a(t)$ is a promising direction for analytical buffer sizing.
    \item \textbf{Scaling to Enterprise Catalogs:} While our fifteen-scenario verification suite spans canonical mediation patterns and a FACE/SOSA-inspired avionics holdout, industrial repositories can contain hundreds of UDDL entities and legacy interface definitions. Combining Graph Neural Network (GNN) policy encoders---which provide relational inductive biases for graph-structured state~\cite{Battaglia2018GN}---over multiplex UDDL embeddings~\cite{mckibben2026multiplex, mckibben2026alignment} with hierarchical uLSC composition will be critical for scaling synthesis to enterprise-level system-of-systems architectures.
\end{enumerate}
