\section{Discussion} \label{sec:discussion}

A central implication of this work is that achieving rapid capability integration under a MOSA requires enhancing interface specifications with semantic-behavioral contracts with executable semantics.  Traditional Interface Control Documents (ICDs) and data-model alignments verify what we formalize as Tier~1 compatibility: ensuring that Fields are structurally present and matched in Color $\kappa$ (including UDDL $(C, L, P)$, runtime representation $\rho$, and provenance).  As our empirical evaluation demonstrates across Scenarios S2--S7 and S15 (Tables~\ref{tab:offline-rl} and~\ref{tab:offline-holdout}), however, Tier~1 Color alignment and Tier~2 kinematic compatibility are necessary but fundamentally insufficient for system integration.  A mediation pipeline that matches every $\kappa$ attribute and bridges push-to-pull transfer modes can nonetheless fail catastrophically at the trajectory level (e.g., by retaining stale samples across quiet intervals, introducing phase lag that violates downstream control deadlines, or misordering non-commutative physical operands).

By unifying a semantic-behavioral Color space with Wymore's T3SD coupling algebra and uLSCs, \emph{The Integration Game} makes these cross-cutting integration failure modes decidable.  Rather than relying on manual authoring discipline or post-hoc integration testing to catch behavioral discrepancies, the Winning Condition embeds Color typing, kinematic flow compatibility, and trajectory-level contract satisfaction into a formal verification procedure.

\subsection{Cost Structure and Learning-Guided Architecture Search} \label{sec:disc-cost}

In classical systems engineering trade studies, evaluating candidate architectures requires balancing hard feasibility constraints against multi-objective performance and cost figures of merit~\cite{Wymore1993}.  When automated search or reinforcement learning agents optimize a composite scalar objective, poorly structured cost functionals risks reward hacking: an agent may leave Consumer Needs unconnected to avoid operator placement costs, instantiate disconnected interior blocks that artificially reduce naive semantic distance metrics, or accept minor behavioral contract breaches in exchange for lower static complexity.

The axioms developed in Section~\ref{sec:meth-cost} prevent these pathologies structurally. Grounding Tier~1 and Tier~2 frontier potentials strictly on the live offer frontier $\mathcal{F}_{\text{live}}$ (Axiom A2) ensures that interior Mediation Operators cannot reduce potential unless they are causally connected to upstream Producers. Route-wiring continuity (Axiom A4) and monotonic potential reduction (Axiom A3) provide a smooth, dense shaping landscape for multi-hop graph assembly, while the lexicographic breach margin $\Omega$ (Axiom A1) guarantees that no static or dynamic cost savings can compensate for a Tier~3 Hot Breach. As the ablation results in Table~\ref{tab:ablations} confirm, both the Markovian progress signals supplied by the uLSC Reward Machine and the lexicographic separation enforced by $\Omega$ are essential for sample-efficient convergence to valid mediation architectures. Moreover, parameterizing the policy over ontology-grounded relational compatibility features $\phi(s, a)$ enables zero-shot structural transfer to unseen component catalogs (Table~\ref{tab:offline-holdout}), demonstrating that the agent learns generalizable rules of semantic and kinematic mediation rather than memorizing scenario-specific wiring sequences.

\subsection{Decidability Boundaries} \label{sec:disc-decidability}

Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-decidable} establishes first, when $TYR_{\mathcal{K}}$ comprises finite-state components over finite platform alphabets $A_{\kappa}$, the Coupled Resultant $Z_b$ is a finite-state Moore automaton; its product with the uLSC monitor $\mathcal{M}_{\text{LSC}}$ has a finite state space, so Breach reachability (and therefore $(Z_b, s_0) \models_T \mathcal{L}(IOR)$ / $(Z_b, s_0) \models_T IOR$) sis decidable even for infinite horizons $OLR = \infty$.  Finite technologies are therefore never an undecidability barrier for universal behavioral certification.

Second, decidability in principle does not yield tractability in practice.  Exhaustive enumeration of alphabets is generally infeasible, and restricting evaluation to a finite suite $ITR_{\text{test}}$ exposes coincidence-point overfitting (Table~\ref{tab:scale}'s sub-millisecond rollouts remain essential for RL, but are incomplete certificates).  Idealized transforms over $\mathbb{R}$ can leave the general nonlinear unbounded case undecidable.  Restricting interior Mediation Operators to bounded memory and Tarski-decidable fragments ($\mathsf{QF\_LRA}$, $\mathsf{QF\_NRA}$, $\mathsf{QF\_LIRA}$) restores effective symbolic certification.  Pairing fast concrete rollouts with a terminal CEGIS gate~\cite{SolarLezama2008Sketch, AlurEtAl2013SyGuS} therefore bridges sample-efficient learning and exact fragment verification (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}), with SMT backends such as Z3 relegated to the Results implementation.

\subsection{Threats to Validity and Future Directions} \label{sec:disc-limitations}

Several methodological boundaries define the scope of the present study and point toward natural extensions:
\begin{enumerate}[topsep=2pt, itemsep=1pt, leftmargin=1.8em]
    \item \textbf{Discrete Synchronous Time Base:} Following Wymore's discrete-system formulation, the Coupled Resultant $Z_b = \mathrm{RSY}(\mathcal{P})$ evolves over a synchronous global tick. Extending the game to continuous-time or asynchronous discrete-event specifications (e.g., via DEVS~\cite{ZeiglerPraehoferKim2000, wach2021devs} or timed automata) will allow finer-grained modeling of network jitter and clock drift across distributed buses.
    \item \textbf{Impulsive Flow Kinematics:} Within the Flow Kinematics tuple $\mathbf{k} = (x, v, a)$, flow acceleration $a$ serves in our current formulation as an architectural indicator capturing impulsive mode transitions across stores and pumps and rate-mismatch exposure across multi-rate boundaries. Developing continuous fluid-queue or network-calculus bounds driven directly by $a(t)$ is a promising direction for analytical buffer sizing.
    \item \textbf{Scaling to Enterprise Catalogs:} While our fifteen-scenario verification suite spans canonical mediation patterns and a FACE/SOSA-inspired avionics holdout, industrial repositories can contain hundreds of UDDL entities and legacy interface definitions. Combining Graph Neural Network (GNN) policy encoders---which provide relational inductive biases for graph-structured state~\cite{Battaglia2018GN}---over multiplex UDDL embeddings~\cite{mckibben2026multiplex, mckibben2026alignment} with hierarchical uLSC composition will be critical for scaling synthesis to enterprise-level system-of-systems architectures.
\end{enumerate}
