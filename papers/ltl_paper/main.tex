\begin{abstract}
\sloppy
In Wymore's Tricotyledon Theory of System Design, an input/output requirement $R$ determines the functionality cotyledon $\mathrm{FSR}(R)$. Each functional design packages a system, a designated state, a time scale, and evidence that the system satisfies $R$. We derive from $R$ a characteristic boundary fragment $\Phi_R$ whose assertional functionality space is canonically equivalent to $\mathrm{FSR}(R)$. The construction covers every Wymorian IOR; structured relations yield compact temporal formulas suitable for established checking methods. A chosen functional design may also serve as an implementation reference. Compiling the transition and readout tables of its system component $Z_{\mathrm{ref}}$ gives a dynamics fragment $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ whose satisfaction is equivalent to existence of a Wymore homomorphism. Invariance and composition results allow this fragment to be reused across state re-encoding, port relabeling, and componentwise elaboration. A four-component streaming run-length encoder demonstrates the method, including the pipeline latency induced by Wymore's synchronous coupling. The case also records the limits: the dynamics fragment is lock-step, excludes eventual-completion claims, and supports automated checking through finite or symbolic presentations.

\keywords{Tricotyledon Theory of System Design (T3SD), temporal logic, formal verification, Model-Based Systems Engineering (MBSE), system homomorphism.}
\end{abstract}
