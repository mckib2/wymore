\begin{abstract}
\sloppy
Adequately verifying that a buildable system realizes stated functional intent is a core concern of systems engineering. In A.\ Wayne Wymore's Tricotyledon Theory of System Design (T3SD), classical implementability is established constructively by exhibiting a system homomorphism from a buildable system to a reference system in the Functionality Cotyledon. We contend that the same functional intent can be expressed as a dynamics-encoding temporal fragment compiled from a Wymore discrete system, and that---on that fragment---assertional satisfaction is equivalent to classical implementability. Engineers may therefore treat property checking of the compiled fragment as the primary verification artifact rather than constructing and maintaining an explicit homomorphism. We give a canonical first-order LTL encoding of discrete-system execution, prove the fragment bi-implication, and illustrate the methodology on a ones-counter system, a finite string recognizer, and a real-valued accumulator under a discrete clock. Checking remains decidable on finite projections and, more generally, requires bounded or theory-restricted queries; we do not claim a decision procedure for arbitrary infinite or real FO-LTL.

\keywords{Tricotyledon Theory of System Design (T3SD), temporal logic, formal verification, Model-Based Systems Engineering (MBSE), system homomorphism.}
\end{abstract}
