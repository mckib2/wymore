\begin{abstract}
\sloppy
Adequately verifying that a buildable system realizes stated functional intent is a core concern of systems engineering. In the Tricotyledon Theory of System Design (T3SD), classical implementability is established constructively as a system homomorphism from a buildable system to a reference system in the Functionality Cotyledon.  We show that the same functional intent can be expressed as an assertional dynamics-encoding temporal fragment compiled from a Wymore discrete system, and that satisfaction over the fragment is equivalent to T3SD implementability. System modelers may therefore treat property checking of the compiled fragment as the primary verification artifact rather than constructing and maintaining explicit homomorphisms. We give a canonical first-order LTL encoding of discrete-system execution, prove that the fragment satisfaction over a buildable system iff there exists a Wymorean homomorphism, and illustrate the methodology on a several case studies over families of systems with discrete and continuous states.

\keywords{Tricotyledon Theory of System Design (T3SD), temporal logic, formal verification, Model-Based Systems Engineering (MBSE), system homomorphism.}
\end{abstract}
