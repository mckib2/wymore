\section{Methodology} \label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Reinterpreting the Functionality Cotyledon}
Figure~\ref{fig:cotyledons-bridge} illustrates the structural relationship between the three cotyledons and the temporal logic correspondence developed in this section. On the left, an input/output requirement $IOR$ generates the functionality cotyledon $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)=\mathrm{FSR}(IOR)$, from which a functional system design $D_f=(Z_f,s_0,T)$ may be selected. In classical T3SD, verification is \emph{constructive}: certifying that $(D_f,Z_b)$ belongs to the implementability cotyledon $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR,TYR)$ requires selecting an explicit state machine $Z_f\in\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ and manually finding a surjective homomorphism $h:Z_m\twoheadrightarrow Z_f$. The proposed approach replaces manual homomorphism construction with two complementary \emph{assertional} verification mechanisms in temporal logic \citep{Pnueli1977,MannaPnueli1995}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Boundary-level verification ($\Phi_{IOR}$):} Every $IOR$ translates into an isomorphic boundary temporal specification $\Phi_{IOR}$ expressing the eligibility relation directly over external input and output trajectories. A system mode $Z_m\in\mathrm{SYSMO}(Z_b)$ on matching boundary alphabets admits an identity-boundary homomorphism onto a functional design in $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ if and only if $Z_m$ satisfies $\Phi_{IOR}$ directly ($Z_m\models_{IO}\Phi_{IOR}$) (see Theorem~6.58 in \citet{Wymore1993}).
    \item \textbf{Reference-dynamics verification ($\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$):} When a specific reference state machine $Z_{\mathrm{ref}}\in\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ has been defined (or when boundary ports require non-identity projections $h_I,h_O$), compiling each entry of the transition and readout tables of $Z_{\mathrm{ref}}$ into a step-local temporal safety rule produces a reference-dynamics fragment $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}(Z_{\mathrm{ref}})$ whose satisfaction $Z_m\models_{\mathrm{dyn}}\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}(Z_{\mathrm{ref}})$ is equivalent to the existence of a surjective homomorphism $h:Z_m\twoheadrightarrow Z_{\mathrm{ref}}$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Isomorphism Between \texorpdfstring{$IOR$s}{IORs} and Temporal Specifications}
\label{sec:trace-observability}
Let $IOR=(OLR,IR,ITR,OR,OTR,ER)$ be any input/output requirement, and let $T_{IOR}\subseteq\mathbb{N}$ be the operational time scale determined by $OLR$. We define its \emph{characteristic boundary temporal specification} as the triple
\begin{equation}
  \Phi_{IOR}=(T_{IOR},\,ITR,\,A_{IOR}),
  \qquad
  A_{IOR}(f,g)\;\Longleftrightarrow\;g\in ER(f),
  \label{eq:phi-ior-def}
\end{equation}
where $A_{IOR}(f,g)$ is a state-free temporal trajectory predicate over complete input trajectories $f\in IR^T$ and output trajectories $g\in OR^T$. Here $\Phi_{IOR}$ omits the standalone $OTR$ component of the 6-tuple without loss of information, because Wymore's $IOR$ axioms guarantee that $OTR$ is the union of the eligible output sets $\bigcup_{f\in ITR}ER(f)$.

Whereas the functionality cotyledon $\mathrm{FSR}(IOR)=\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ is defined in set-theoretic terms, temporal logic defines the class of conforming systems assertionally as the set of models satisfying $\Phi_{IOR}$. Specifically, for any discrete system $Z=(S,IR,OR,\delta,\rho)$, initial state $s_0\in S$, and nonempty operational time scale $T\subseteq T_{IOR}$, we say that $(Z,s_0,T)$ \emph{assertionally satisfies} $\Phi_{IOR}$, written $Z,s_0,T\models_{IO}\Phi_{IOR}$ (or simply $Z\models_{IO}\Phi_{IOR}$ when $s_0$ and $T$ are fixed by context), when for every admissible input $f\in ITR$ and every input trajectory $h$ agreeing with $f$ on $T_{IOR}$, there exists an output trajectory $g$ satisfying $A_{IOR}(f,g)$ such that the generated output of $Z$ on $h$ from $s_0$ agrees with $g$ at every tick $t\in T$. We call the set of all such satisfying triples $(Z,s_0,T)$ the \emph{assertional functionality space}, denoted $\mathrm{AFSR}(\Phi_{IOR})$.

\begin{theorem}[Boundary $IOR$--Temporal Logic Isomorphism]
\label{thm:boundary-characteristic}
For every input/output requirement $IOR$, discrete system $Z$, initial state $s_0$, and operational time scale $T$,
\[
 Z,s_0,T\models_{IO}\Phi_{IOR}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathrm{SatisfiesIOR}(Z,IOR,s_0,T).
\]
Consequently, there is a canonical bijection between the assertional functionality space and the functionality cotyledon,
\[
  \mathrm{AFSR}(\Phi_{IOR})\;\simeq\;\mathrm{FSR}(IOR)=\mathrm{CTL}(IOR),
\]
that acts as the identity on the underlying system $Z$, initial state $s_0$, and operational time scale $T$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Sketch: Unfolding the definition of $A_{IOR}(f,g)\Leftrightarrow g\in ER(f)$ inside $Z,s_0,T\models_{IO}\Phi_{IOR}$ yields the exact trajectory quantification of Wymore's $\mathrm{SatisfiesIOR}$ (Definition~6.31 of \citet{Wymore1993}). Because the satisfaction condition is identical for every triple $(Z,s_0,T)$, pairing $(Z,s_0,T)$ with its satisfaction proof establishes a one-to-one correspondence between $\mathrm{AFSR}(\Phi_{IOR})$ and $\mathrm{FSR}(IOR)$. A full treatment is found in Appendix~\ref{app:hom-biimp}.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:boundary-characteristic} holds for arbitrary operational time scales (finite or infinite), restricted input domains $ITR$, and deterministic or nondeterministic eligibility relations $ER$. Assertional satisfaction $Z,s_0,T\models_{IO}\Phi_{IOR}$ evaluates eligibility only on the designated operational time scale $T\subseteq T_{IOR}$ and therefore does not require infinite-trace LTL semantics. When a model checker expects infinite paths, a finite operational length may be realized by restricting evaluation to $T$, by finite-trace LTL ($\mathrm{LTL}_f$), or by appending a terminal stuttering sink; the automated pipeline and case study of this paper use infinite $T_{IOR}$ with $G$-safety formulas over infinite runs of finite system modes. Expressing $A_{IOR}(f,g)$ as a temporal logic formula over input and output trajectories also clarifies when $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ is empty versus infinite. For example, an instantaneous-echo requirement $G(y(t)=f(t))$ over binary inputs demands $y(0)=f(0)$ at tick zero. Because the initial Moore readout $y(0)=\rho(s_0)$ is fixed by $s_0$ before $f(0)$ is processed, no discrete system can satisfy the formula for both $f(0)=0$ and $f(0)=1$, immediately exposing why $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ is empty. Conversely, once a temporal specification accounts for at least a one-tick state-transition latency, such as $y(0)=b_0\wedge G(X\,y(t)=f(t))$, a two-state shift register satisfies $\Phi_{IOR}$ and $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ contains infinitely many state-space realizations.

\subsection{Direct Mode-to-\texorpdfstring{$IOR$}{IOR} Verification}
\label{sec:mode-ior-bridge}
We now connect the boundary temporal specification $\Phi_{IOR}$ to the buildability cotyledon $\mathrm{CTL}(TYR)$ and the implementability cotyledon $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR,TYR)$. Suppose a candidate architecture $Z_b=\mathrm{RSY}(SCR)\in\mathrm{CTL}(TYR)$ has been assembled from components in $TYR$, and let $Z_m\in\mathrm{SYSMO}(Z_b)$ be an operational system mode of $Z_b$.

In classical T3SD, requirement satisfaction is defined as a property of abstract functional designs in $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$, and Wymore's Theorem~6.58 transfers $\mathrm{SatisfiesIOR}$ along an identity-boundary homomorphism ($h_I=\mathrm{id}_{IR}$, $h_O=\mathrm{id}_{OR}$). Combining Theorem~6.58 with Theorem~\ref{thm:boundary-characteristic} shows that searching for an intermediate functional state machine $Z_f$ and a homomorphism $h:Z_m\twoheadrightarrow Z_f$ can be bypassed when verifying a system mode:

\begin{theorem}[Direct Mode-to-$IOR$ Implementability]
\label{thm:mode-ior-bridge}
Let $Z_b=\mathrm{RSY}(SCR)\in\mathrm{CTL}(TYR)$ be a buildable resultant in technology $TYR$, and let $Z_m=(S_m,IR,OR,\delta_m,\rho_m)\in\mathrm{SYSMO}(Z_b)$ be a system mode of $Z_b$ with initial state $s_{m,0}\in S_m$ and operational time scale $T\subseteq T_{IOR}$. Then the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item There exists a functional system design $D_f=(Z_f,s_{f,0},T)\in\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ and an identity-boundary homomorphism $h=(h_S,\mathrm{id}_{IR},\mathrm{id}_{OR}):Z_m\twoheadrightarrow Z_f$ with $h_S(s_{m,0})=s_{f,0}$.
    \item The system mode satisfies the characteristic boundary temporal specification directly:
    \[
        Z_m,s_{m,0},T\;\models_{IO}\;\Phi_{IOR}.
    \]
\end{enumerate}
Whenever either condition holds, $Z_b$ implements $D_f$ via the mode $Z_m$ and forms an implementable system design $(D_f,Z_b)\in\mathrm{CTL}(IOR,TYR)=\mathrm{ISR}(IOR,TYR)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Sketch: $(1\Rightarrow 2)$: Suppose $h:Z_m\twoheadrightarrow Z_f$ is a Wymore homomorphism with $h_I=\mathrm{id}_{IR}$, $h_O=\mathrm{id}_{OR}$, and $h_S(s_{m,0})=s_{f,0}$, where $D_f=(Z_f,s_{f,0},T)\in\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$. Because $h$ commutes with $\delta$ and $\rho$ at every step, induction on time $t$ shows that $Z_m$ initialized at $s_{m,0}$ and $Z_f$ initialized at $s_{f,0}$ generate identical boundary output trajectories $y(t)$ for every input trajectory $f$. By Wymore's Theorem~6.58, $Z_m$ therefore satisfies $IOR$, which by Theorem~\ref{thm:boundary-characteristic} is equivalent to $Z_m,s_{m,0},T\models_{IO}\Phi_{IOR}$.

$(2\Rightarrow 1)$: Conversely, if $Z_m,s_{m,0},T\models_{IO}\Phi_{IOR}$, then by Theorem~\ref{thm:boundary-characteristic}, $D_m:=(Z_m,s_{m,0},T)$ is itself a member of $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$. The identity homomorphism $\mathrm{id}:Z_m\twoheadrightarrow Z_m$ witnesses that $Z_m$ is a homomorphic image of the mode $Z_m\in\mathrm{SYSMO}(Z_b)$, so $Z_b$ implements $D_m$ (Wymore Definition~5.71) and $(D_m,Z_b)\in\mathrm{CTL}(IOR,TYR)$.

A full treatment is found in Appendix~\ref{app:hom-biimp}.
\end{proof}

Thus boundary verification $Z_m\models_{IO}\Phi_{IOR}$ certifies implementability of \emph{some} functional design in $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ (namely the mode itself as $D_f$) as required by Definition~5.71 and Theorem~6.58 in \citet{Wymore1993}. If the engineering task instead requires conformance to a \emph{prescribed} reference machine $Z_{\mathrm{ref}}\in\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$, the dynamics-fragment check developed below in Theorem~\ref{thm:dynamics-characterization} is applicable.

When the mode $Z_m$ uses concrete port encodings $(I_m,O_m)$ that relate to the requirement alphabets $(IR,OR)$ via interface maps $h_I:I_m\twoheadrightarrow IR$ and $h_O:O_m\twoheadrightarrow OR$ (for example, projecting a single unconnected port from a coupled port vector), pulling $\Phi_{IOR}$ back along $(h_I,h_O)$ corresponds to Wymore's transported requirement ($\mathrm{HIMIO}(IOR,h_I,h_O)$, Definition~6.72 of \citet{Wymore1993}), allowing Theorem~\ref{thm:mode-ior-bridge} to be applied directly on the mode's boundary ports.

\subsection{Reference-Dynamics Fragment \texorpdfstring{$\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$}{Phi\_dyn}}
\label{sec:dynamics-encoding-fragment}
Classical constructive T3SD certifies implementability by pairing a buildable design with an explicit functional system design $D_{\mathrm{ref}}=(Z_{\mathrm{ref}},s_0,T)\in\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ linked by a surjective homomorphism (Definitions~5.71, 6.40, and~7.64 of \citet{Wymore1993}). Whenever such a baseline reference machine $Z_{\mathrm{ref}}=(S_{\mathrm{ref}},I_{\mathrm{ref}},O_{\mathrm{ref}},\delta_{\mathrm{ref}},\rho_{\mathrm{ref}})$ has been selected and the engineer wishes to verify that a candidate mode $Z_m\in\mathrm{SYSMO}(Z_b)$ is a homomorphic elaboration of it, we compile the transition and readout tables of $Z_{\mathrm{ref}}$ into a \emph{dynamics-encoding fragment} $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}(Z_{\mathrm{ref}})$, summarized in Table~\ref{tab:phi-dyn-fragment}.

Given candidate maps $(h_S,h_I,h_O)$ from $Z_m$ to $Z_{\mathrm{ref}}$, we write $\sigma_s$ for the atomic state proposition $h_S(g(t))=s$ at tick $t$, $\iota_i$ (and $\iota_\varnothing$) for the input proposition $h_I(f(t))=i$ (and autonomous input $\mathrm{none}$), and $\omega_o$ (and $\omega_\varnothing$) for the readout proposition $h_O(y(t))=o$ (and silent readout $\mathrm{none}$). We write $Z_m\models_{\mathrm{dyn}}\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}(Z_{\mathrm{ref}})$ when there exist surjective maps $(h_S,h_I,h_O)$ under which every run of $Z_m$ satisfies the four $G$/$X$ clause families of Table~\ref{tab:phi-dyn-fragment}. Here the $G$/$X$ formulas encode step-local transition and readout preservation, while surjectivity of the witness maps $(h_S,h_I,h_O)$ is a separate condition from Definition~4.3 in \citet{Wymore1993}, discharged by enumeration or SAT encoding of the maps rather than by liveness formulas such as $\bigwedge_{s\in S_{\mathrm{ref}}}F\,\sigma_s$ inside the safety fragment. (When $Z_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is taken as a reachable operational mode, that check coincides with coverage of every reference state.)

\begin{theorem}[Dynamics-Fragment Characterization of Homomorphisms]
\label{thm:dynamics-characterization}
Fix a functional design $D_{\mathrm{ref}}\in\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ with system component $Z_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and a system mode $Z_m\in\mathrm{SYSMO}(Z_b)$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
  Z_m \models_{\mathrm{dyn}} \Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}(Z_{\mathrm{ref}})
  \;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;
  &\exists\, h : Z_m \twoheadrightarrow Z_{\mathrm{ref}}\\
  &\text{Wymore system homomorphism}.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Sketch: By definition, $Z_m\models_{\mathrm{dyn}}\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}(Z_{\mathrm{ref}})$ asserts the existence of surjective maps $(h_S,h_I,h_O)$ under which every run of $Z_m$ satisfies the four $G$/$X$ clause families in Table~\ref{tab:phi-dyn-fragment}. If $h=(h_S,h_I,h_O):Z_m\twoheadrightarrow Z_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is a Wymore homomorphism, its readout and transition equations~\eqref{eq:wymore-hom-def} hold at every state of $Z_m$, so those clause families hold along every run. Conversely, given such surjective maps, initializing a run at an arbitrary state $x\in S_m$ and inspecting tick $t=0$ recovers the static readout equation $h_O(\rho_m(x))=\rho_{\mathrm{ref}}(h_S(x))$ and the one-step transition equation $h_S(\delta_m(x,p))=\delta_{\mathrm{ref}}(h_S(x),h_I(p))$. Appendix~\ref{app:hom-biimp} provides the full derivation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Implementability Cotyledon Membership]
\label{cor:cotyledon-implementability}
If $Z_b=\mathrm{RSY}(SCR)\in\mathrm{CTL}(TYR)$ is buildable in technology $TYR$ and possesses a system mode $Z_m\in\mathrm{SYSMO}(Z_b)$ such that $Z_m\models_{\mathrm{dyn}}\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}(Z_{\mathrm{ref}})$ for $D_{\mathrm{ref}}\in\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$, then $Z_b$ implements $D_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and $(D_{\mathrm{ref}},Z_b)\in\mathrm{CTL}(IOR,TYR)=\mathrm{ISR}(IOR,TYR)$.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{LTL Model Checking for Finite Buildable Modes}
\label{sec:ltl-decidability}
While an idealized functional specification in $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ may employ unbounded mathematical types (such as an infinite tape or an unbounded counter in $\mathbb{N}$) and always admits infinitely many state-space realizations, physical digital and embedded software components in a real-world technology catalog $TYR$ (such as registers, sensors, actuators, communication buffers, and lookup tables) typically have finite state spaces and finite port alphabets \citep{Wymore1993,BurchClarkeMcMillanDill1992,BaierKatoen2008}. Because the state space of a coupled resultant $Z_b=\mathrm{RSY}(SCR)$ is the Cartesian product $\prod_{i=1}^n S_i$ of its component state spaces, any resultant assembled from finite components in $TYR$ is a finite discrete system, and every operational system mode $Z_m\in\mathrm{SYSMO}(Z_b)$ isolated from $Z_b$ is a finite Kripke structure. Over a finite system mode $Z_m$, any boundary requirement $\Phi_{IOR}$ expressed in propositional LTL (as well as any finite-state dynamics fragment $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}(Z_{\mathrm{ref}})$) is decidable via standard model checking and SAT/SMT solving \citep{BaierKatoen2008,BiereCimattiClarkeZhu1999,deMouraZ32008}. Rather than solving an NP-hard homomorphism search by hand or maintaining state-space projections across architectural revisions, the engineer evaluates $Z_m\models_{IO}\Phi_{IOR}$ (or $Z_m\models_{\mathrm{dyn}}\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}(Z_{\mathrm{ref}})$) automatically: a passing check certifies membership in $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR,TYR)$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:mode-ior-bridge} or Theorem~\ref{thm:dynamics-characterization}, whereas a failing check identifies the violated temporal clause.
