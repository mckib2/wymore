\subsection{Verification Paradigm Comparison}
Figure~\ref{fig:comparison} contrasts classical constructive verification with assertional property checking. In panel~A, requirements are formalized as a reference system $Z_{\text{spec}}$ and a buildable system $Z_{\text{impl}}$ is related to it by a Wymore system homomorphism. In panel~B, requirements are encoded as a property set $\Phi$ (directly, or optionally via $Z_{\text{spec}}$) and $Z_{\text{impl}}$ is checked by assertional satisfaction. Table~\ref{tab:comparison} summarizes the comparison. When a buildable system changes structure, constructive proofs that depend on a particular homomorphism may need substantial rework; assertional checking re-evaluates whether the revised $Z_{\text{impl}}$ still satisfies the same $\Phi$.

\subsection{Traces, Observability, and Assertional Modes}
\label{sec:trace-observability}
For $Z = (S, I, O, \delta, \rho)$ and trajectories $(f, g, y)$, Wymore's construction is
\begin{align}
    g(0) &= s_0 \label{eq:trace-init}\\
    g(t+1) &= \delta(g(t), f(t)) \label{eq:trace-step}\\
    y(t) &= \rho(g(t)) \label{eq:trace-readout}
\end{align}
with execution trace $\sigma = (f, g, y)$. Properties may refer to external ports ($f$, $y$) or internal state ($g$).

We write $Z \models_{\mathcal{L}} \Phi$ when every admissible input trajectory and initial state in a stated class induces a trace satisfying $\Phi$ \citep{Pnueli1977,BaierKatoen2008}. The assertional Functionality Cotyledon is $\mathcal{FC}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Phi) = \{ Z \mid Z \models_{\mathcal{L}} \Phi \}$. Verification of a buildable system is the membership test $Z_{\text{impl}} \models_{\mathcal{L}} \Phi$. Admissible inputs are those consistent with boundary assumptions, not necessarily all of $I^T$ \citep{salado2019mbse}.

\label{sec:assertional-modes}
Assertional checking admits two modeling strategies: systems modelers may write $\Phi$ directly, or compile $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ from an explicit reference $Z_{\text{spec}}$. The property set/homomoprhism bi-implication depends on \emph{fragment shape}, not on the modeling strategy. A complete dynamics-encoding property set (whether compiled from $Z_{\text{spec}}$ or authored in the same clause schemas) is equivalent to implementability relative to the (possibly implied) reference system. Temporal formulas outside that fragment, such as bare liveness $F$, are however not.

\subsection{The Dynamics-Encoding Fragment}
\label{sec:dynamics-encoding-fragment}

When assertional checking is used to answer the classical implementability question---whether a buildable system is a Wymore homomorphic image of a reference system---$\Phi$ cannot be an arbitrary temporal formula. We use a \emph{dynamics-encoding} fragment $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$: a direct logical reading of the state-transition and readout table of $Z_{\text{spec}}$.

Table~\ref{tab:phi-dyn-fragment} states the fragment that makes the bi-implication hold. Clauses are compiled from $Z_{\text{spec}}$ and checked on $Z_{\text{impl}}$ under surjective maps $h_S,h_I,h_O$, or under identity when labels are shared. Computationally: at each tick of a buildable system's run, $\mathit{state}(s)$ means the buildable system's own state component equals $s$; $\mathit{specState}(s)$ means the projected specification state equals $s$, i.e., $h_S$ of the current buildable system's state is $s$. Inputs and outputs are read the same way on the buildable system's trace (with $h_I$/$h_O$ when checking under a homomorphism).

Because clauses are predicate-indexed rather than flattened into a finite propositional list, $S$, $I$, and $O$ may be infinite or real-valued. Closed readouts and autonomous steps are first-class, so silent outputs and unguided ticks cannot be ignored by a buildable system that claims to implement $Z_{\text{spec}}$.

\paragraph{Bi-implication with classical implementability.}
For fixed $Z_{\text{spec}}$, the following is proved in Appendix~\ref{app:hom-biimp} and illustrated in Section~\ref{sec:case-study}:
\[
  Z_{\text{impl}} \models_{\mathrm{dyn}} \Phi_{Z_{\text{spec}}}
  \;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;
  \exists\, h : Z_{\text{impl}} \to Z_{\text{spec}}\ \text{Wymore system homomorphism}.
\]
Here $\models_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ means every admissible run of $Z_{\text{impl}}$ obeys the clause families of Table~\ref{tab:phi-dyn-fragment} under some surjective projection. No finiteness or total-readout precondition is required. On this fragment, engineers may treat assertional satisfaction of $\Phi_{Z_{\text{spec}}}$ as the primary verification artifact.

\paragraph{Shared fixed-table specialization.}
When $Z_{\text{spec}}$ and $Z_{\text{impl}}$ share $(S,I,O)$ and labels are treated as canonical, literal satisfaction is equivalent to pointwise agreement of $\delta$ and $\rho$. Cross-type elaborations require the full hom-relative reading above. Finite propositional projections support automated model checking when spaces are small; they specialize the same semantic content and do not weaken the bi-implication.


\subsection{Property Checking}
\label{sec:automated-checking}

Property checking of $Z_{\text{impl}} \models_{\mathcal{L}} \Phi$ may proceed by finite-horizon unrolling, finite-state abstraction, or theory-specific solvers on bounded queries \citep{BaierKatoen2008,deMouraZ32008}. Satisfaction of $\Phi$ guarantees only what $\Phi$ states. Unbounded FO-LTL over infinite or real-valued coordinates is not decidable in general; practitioners rely on finite projections, bounded horizons, or restricted input classes. Propositional LTL on finite abstractions remains a mature automated path when those abstractions are adequate for the claim at hand.
