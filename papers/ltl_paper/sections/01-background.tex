\section{Background} \label{sec:background}

\subsection{Tricotyledon Theory of System Design}
Wymore's T3SD organizes system design into three cotyledons: Functionality (FC), Buildability (BC), and Implementability (IC) \citep{Wymore1993}. The FC contains designs that meet required input/output behavior (and performance orderings). BC captures technology and cost constraints. IC comprises buildable designs that map homomorphically onto functional ones. Classically, requirements are formalized as a reference system $Z_{\text{spec}}$ in the FC, and a buildable system $Z_{\text{impl}}$ is verified by exhibiting a Wymore system homomorphism. This paper focuses on the FC.

Figure~\ref{fig:t3sd-cotyledons} places both readings in the cotyledon layout. Classical success places $Z_{\text{impl}}$ in the implementability overlap. Assertional checking asks whether $Z_{\text{impl}} \models_{\mathcal{L}} \Phi$.

We restrict attention to Wymore discrete systems (DSYSTEMS): deterministic 5-tuples $Z=(S,I,O,\delta,\rho)$ \citep{Wymore1993}. ``Discrete'' refers to the clock; $S$, $I$, and $O$ may be infinite or real-valued at each tick. Given $f:T\to I$ and $s_0\in S$, Wymore's trajectory theorems yield unique state and output trajectories. Systems of interest are open: verification concerns observable boundary behavior \citep{salado2019mbse}.

Conventions: $Z_{\text{spec}}$ is the reference system; $Z_{\text{impl}}$ is the buildable (potentially implementable) system under verification; $h$ denotes a Wymore system homomorphism; $\Phi$ is a property set in dialect $\mathcal{L}$; $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ is reserved for the dynamics-encoding fragment.

\subsection{Temporal Logics}
Temporal logics state requirements over time-indexed traces \citep{BaierKatoen2008,Pnueli1977}. Table~\ref{tab:ltl-operators} lists the operators that appear in this paper, aligned with the dynamics-encoding fragment of Section~\ref{sec:dynamics-encoding-fragment}. First-order LTL (FO-LTL) admits quantifiers over state, input, or output at each tick: it supplies the ambient dialect for predicate-indexed $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ atoms and for formulas outside the fragment. A comparison of $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ with propositional LTL and FO-LTL appears in Table~\ref{tab:fragment-compare}.

\subsection{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

This section positions the dynamics-encoding bridge relative to neighboring frameworks. The intent is comparative clarity, not a comprehensive survey.

\paragraph{T3SD and Wymore modernization.}
Wymore's T3SD remains the host theory for this paper \citep{Wymore1993}. Recent work reconnects that framework to SysML state machines and to DEVS as a path toward scientific foundations for MBSE \citep{salado2019wymore,wach2021devs,salado2019mbse}. We do not replace the cotyledon structure. We reinterpret Functionality Cotyledon verification so that a compiled temporal fragment can stand in for maintaining an explicit homomorphism as the primary artifact.

\paragraph{Temporal logic and model checking.}
Linear temporal logic and model checking provide mature machinery for assertional properties over traces \citep{Pnueli1977,BaierKatoen2008}. Our contribution is not a new model-checking algorithm. It is a restricted dynamics-encoding fragment whose satisfaction, for a fixed reference system, is equivalent to classical Wymore implementability. General LTL model checking of arbitrary formulas outside that fragment does not by itself supply that equivalence.

\paragraph{Refinement, simulation, and bisimulation.}
Process-algebraic and automata-theoretic refinement notions---including simulation and bisimulation---are the closest cousins of constructive morphisms in concurrent systems \citep{Milner1989,LynchTuttle1987}. Wymore system homomorphism is a surjective projection of transition and readout tables between discrete systems. It is related in spirit to refinement but is not identified here with observational equivalence or weak bisimulation; the bi-implication we prove is stated for the dynamics-encoding fragment and Wymore's homomorphic-image relation.

\paragraph{DEVS and system morphisms.}
DEVS provides a parallel systems-theoretic lineage with its own morphism hierarchy \citep{wach2021devs}. Convergence between T3SD and DEVS is an active research direction. The present paper stays on Wymore discrete systems and Functionality Cotyledon implementability; we do not claim a DEVS morphism theorem.

\paragraph{Hybrid and continuous-time formalisms.}
Differential dynamic logic and signal temporal logic address continuous-time and signal-valued properties \citep{platzer2018logical,MalerNickovic2004}. Our real-valued case study uses a discrete clock with real-valued state and input at ticks. That setting is closer to sampled discrete systems than to hybrid continuous-time semantics, and we do not claim continuous-time verification results.

\paragraph{Contracts, assume-guarantee, and model-based requirements.}
Contract-based and assume-guarantee styles, together with systems-theoretic treatments of true model-based requirements, share an assertional emphasis on properties as carriers of intent \citep{salado2019mbse}. Specification languages such as TLA$^+$ similarly treat system behavior as properties of executions \citep{Lamport2002}. Those approaches are complementary. They do not, by themselves, establish equivalence between property satisfaction and Wymore homomorphic image on a dynamics-encoding fragment.

\paragraph{Runtime verification and supervisory control.}
Runtime verification synthesizes monitors that observe traces against temporal specifications. Supervisory control and discrete-event language specifications address string acceptance and control under partial observation. Our string-recognizer example is adjacent to language-style specifications, but the dynamics-encoding fragment compiles the full transition and readout table for implementability equivalence, rather than synthesizing a supervisor or a runtime monitor alone. Deeper engagement with those literatures is left for future expansion.
