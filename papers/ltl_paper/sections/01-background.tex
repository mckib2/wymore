\section{Background} \label{sec:background}

\subsection{Tricotyledon Theory of System Design}
Wymore's Tricotyledon Theory of System Design (T3SD) partitions design into three cotyledons \citep{Wymore1993}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Functionality.} $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)=\mathrm{FSR}(IOR)$ contains functional system designs: a system, designated state, time scale, and proof of satisfaction of $IOR=(OLR,IR,ITR,OR,OTR,ER)$. For each admissible input trajectory in $ITR$, $ER$ gives the eligible outputs.
    \item \textbf{Buildability.} $\mathrm{CTL}(TYR)=\mathrm{BSR}(TYR)$ contains resultants $Z=\mathrm{RSY}(SCR)$ of recipes whose components belong to technology $TYR$.
    \item \textbf{Implementability.} $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR,TYR)=\mathrm{ISR}(IOR,TYR)$ pairs functional and buildable designs for which the build implements the functional design.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Classical constructive practice.}
To avoid quantifying directly over boundary trajectories, classical practice selects
$D_{\mathrm{ref}}\in\mathrm{FSR}(IOR)$ and exhibits a surjective, transition- and
readout-preserving homomorphism from the buildable resultant to the system component
$Z_{\mathrm{ref}}:=D_{\mathrm{ref}}.Z$. Wymore's Theorem~6.58 transfers requirement
satisfaction when the input and output maps are identities; differing alphabets require
transport along those maps.

We restrict attention to Wymore discrete systems (DSYSTEMS): deterministic 5-tuples $Z=(S,I,O,\delta,\rho)$ \citep{Wymore1993}. ``Discrete'' refers to the clock; $S$, $I$, and $O$ may be infinite or real-valued at each tick. Given $f:T\to I$ and $s_0\in S$, Wymore's trajectory theorems yield unique state and output trajectories. Systems of interest are open: verification concerns observable boundary behavior \citep{salado2019mbse}.

Conventions: $D_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is a chosen functional design in $\mathrm{FSR}(IOR)$,
$Z_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is its system component, and $Z_{\mathrm{impl}}$ is the resultant
of the buildable design under verification. The symbol $h$ denotes a Wymore system
homomorphism, $\Phi_R$ denotes the characteristic boundary fragment of $R$, and
$\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ denotes the dynamics fragment compiled from $Z_{\mathrm{ref}}$.

\subsection{Temporal Logics}
Temporal logics provide notation and checking methods for time-indexed traces
\citep{BaierKatoen2008,Pnueli1977}. This paper identifies two fragments aligned
with Wymore's definitions. The characteristic boundary fragment retains the
predicates and trajectory relation of an IOR. The dynamics fragment uses the
table-shaped operators in Table~\ref{tab:ltl-operators}. Some IOR relations
require symbolic predicates because they lack a finite ordinary-LTL formula.

\subsection{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

Three bodies of work locate the contribution. Safety fragments use invariant,
step-local formulas and support mature checking methods
\citep{Sistla1994,MannaPnueli1995,BiereCimattiClarkeZhu1999}. Characteristic
formulas compile operational models into logical descriptions, commonly up to
simulation or bisimulation \citep{GrafSifakis1986,SteffenIngolfsdottir1994};
here the induced relation is Wymore's homomorphic image, whose maps resemble
bounded morphisms \citep{BlackburnDeRijkeVenema2001}. T3SD modernization, DEVS,
contract-based requirements, and supervisory control provide related
assertional settings \citep{salado2019wymore,wach2021devs,salado2019mbse,RamadgeWonham1989}.
This paper establishes the correspondence with Wymore functionality and
implementation. Searching for finite maps is NP-hard in general
\citep{HellNesetril1990}.
