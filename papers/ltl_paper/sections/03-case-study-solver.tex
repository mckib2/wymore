\subsection{Automated LTL Verification of Buildable System Modes}
\label{sec:case-solver}

Once the finite-capacity technology catalog $TYR_N$ is fixed, the boundary specification $\Phi_{IOR_N}$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:rle-fin-recurrence} compiles into six families of propositional LTL safety clauses. Writing $f\in\{0,1\}$ for the boundary input bit $f(t)$, $(y_{\mathrm{bit}},y_{\mathrm{cnt}})\in\{0,1\}\times\{0,\dots,N\}$ for the boundary register output $y(t)=(\text{bit},\text{count})$, and $c\in\{0,1\}$ for the sense/act head phase $c(t)$, the boundary temporal specification takes the form:
\begin{align}
  \varphi_{\mathrm{init}} &: (y_{\mathrm{bit}}=0 \wedge y_{\mathrm{cnt}}=0) \wedge X(y_{\mathrm{bit}}=0 \wedge y_{\mathrm{cnt}}=0) \nonumber\\[-4pt]
  &\hspace{2.2em} \wedge XX(y_{\mathrm{bit}}=0 \wedge y_{\mathrm{cnt}}=0), \label{eq:ltl-init}\\
  \varphi_{\mathrm{mode}} &: G\bigl((c=0 \leftrightarrow X(c=1)) \wedge (c=1 \leftrightarrow X(c=0))\bigr), \label{eq:ltl-mode}\\
  \varphi_{\mathrm{hold}} &: \bigwedge_{b=0}^{1}\bigwedge_{k=1}^{N} G\bigl((c=1 \wedge y_{\mathrm{bit}}=b \wedge y_{\mathrm{cnt}}=k) \nonumber\\[-4pt]
  &\hspace{3.6em} \rightarrow X(y_{\mathrm{bit}}=b \wedge y_{\mathrm{cnt}}=k)\bigr), \label{eq:ltl-hold}\\
  \varphi_{\mathrm{start}} &: \bigwedge_{b=0}^{1} G\bigl((c=0 \wedge f=b \wedge X(y_{\mathrm{cnt}}=0)) \nonumber\\[-4pt]
  &\hspace{3.6em} \rightarrow XXX(y_{\mathrm{bit}}=b \wedge y_{\mathrm{cnt}}=1)\bigr), \label{eq:ltl-start}\\
  \varphi_{\mathrm{inc}} &: \bigwedge_{b=0}^{1}\bigwedge_{k=1}^{N} G\Bigl(\bigl(c=0 \wedge f=b \wedge X(y_{\mathrm{bit}}=b \wedge y_{\mathrm{cnt}}=k)\bigr) \nonumber\\[-2pt]
  &\hspace{2.6em} \rightarrow XXX\bigl(y_{\mathrm{bit}}=b \wedge y_{\mathrm{cnt}}=\min(k{+}1,N)\bigr)\Bigr), \label{eq:ltl-inc}\\
  \varphi_{\mathrm{reset}} &: \bigwedge_{b=0}^{1}\bigwedge_{k=1}^{N} G\Bigl(\bigl(c=0 \wedge f=b \wedge X(y_{\mathrm{bit}}=\neg b \wedge y_{\mathrm{cnt}}=k)\bigr) \nonumber\\[-2pt]
  &\hspace{2.6em} \rightarrow XXX(y_{\mathrm{bit}}=b \wedge y_{\mathrm{cnt}}=1)\Bigr). \label{eq:ltl-reset}
\end{align}
Each clause corresponds to a specific operational requirement of the RLE pipeline: $\varphi_{\mathrm{init}}$ forces the full idle pair $y=(0,0)$ across the three-tick pipeline startup window ($t=0,1,2$); $\varphi_{\mathrm{mode}}$ enforces the alternating sense/act phase clock; $\varphi_{\mathrm{hold}}$ requires the output register to hold a post-startup run pair ($k\in\{1,\ldots,N\}$) across the second half of each cycle, while the startup hold of $(0,0)$ is already covered by $\varphi_{\mathrm{init}}$; and $\varphi_{\mathrm{start}}$, $\varphi_{\mathrm{inc}}$, and $\varphi_{\mathrm{reset}}$ govern the three-tick end-to-end input-to-output pipeline response ($XXX$) on the first symbol, a matching symbol (saturating at capacity $N$), and a run-breaking symbol, respectively. Together, these six families contribute $1+1+2+3(2N)=6N+4$ propositional LTL clauses at capacity $N$.

We implemented the automated verification workflow in Python (v3.12.3) on Ubuntu 24.04 under Windows Subsystem for Linux 2 (WSL2). Given a candidate component catalog $TYR_N$ and coupling recipe $SCR_N$, the workflow constructs the coupled resultant $Z_{b,N}=\mathrm{RSY}(SCR_N)$, isolates the reachable operational system mode $Z_{m,N}\in\mathrm{SYSMO}(Z_{b,N})$ from the initial reset state, and checks the boundary LTL specification $\Phi_{IOR_N}$ (Equations~\eqref{eq:ltl-init}--\eqref{eq:ltl-reset}) by explicit-state exploration of that mode. Separately, when a prescribed reference $Z_{\mathrm{ref},N}$ is in view, $Z_{m,N}\models_{\mathrm{dyn}}\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}(Z_{\mathrm{ref},N})$ is decided by a propositional SAT encoding of the existential witness $\exists(h_S,h_I,h_O)$ (functionality, readout/step preservation, and surjectivity), discharged by Z3~\cite{deMouraZ32008}; importantly, the maps are discovered by the solver rather than supplied as a hand-written architectural projection. Table~\ref{tab:solver-results} reports the $\Phi_{IOR_N}$ proof-of-concept results across candidate designs. In the nominal four-component architecture, the input tape ($|S_0|=2$), sense/act head ($|S_1|=2$), output register ($|S_2|=2(N{+}1)$), and instruction table ($|S_3|=4(N{+}1)$) form a Cartesian product resultant state space of size $|S_{\mathrm{resultant}}|=32(N{+}1)^2$, whereas synchronous coupling restricts the reachable operational mode $Z_{m,N}$ to a much smaller invariant sub-system $|S_{\mathrm{mode}}|$.

The nine candidate designs in Table~\ref{tab:solver-results} are organized into three evaluation groups that test capacity scaling, subsystem refactoring invariance, and fault and timing-mismatch diagnosis.

The first three rows (\emph{Baseline coupled RLE} for $N=2,3,4$) evaluate how verification scales as the hardware counter capacity $N$ of the technology catalog $TYR_N$ increases. Each design is constructed by interconnecting the nominal input tape ($Z_0$), sense/act head ($Z_1$), output register ($Z_2$), and instruction table ($Z_3$) through the coupling recipe $SCR_N$ of Figure~\ref{fig:rle-architecture}, with the count registers in $Z_2$ and $Z_3$ bounded to $\{0,\dots,N\}$. Increasing $N$ from $2$ to $4$ expands the coupled resultant state space $|S_{\mathrm{resultant}}|$ from $288$ to $800$ states and the reachable operational mode $|S_{\mathrm{mode}}|$ from $31$ to $55$ states ($12N+7$). All three designs pass all $6N+4$ LTL clauses ($16$, $22$, and $28$ clauses, respectively). By Theorem~\ref{thm:mode-ior-bridge}, each PASS verdict on $\Phi_{IOR_N}$ certifies that $(D_{m,N},Z_{b,N})\in\mathrm{CTL}(IOR_N,TYR_N)$ for the mode-as-functional-design $D_{m,N}=(Z_{m,N},s_{b,0},T)$; the SAT-based check $Z_{m,N}\models_{\mathrm{dyn}}\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}(Z_{\mathrm{ref},N})$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:dynamics-characterization} certifies a surjective homomorphism onto the prescribed pipeline reference $Z_{\mathrm{ref},N}$, without requiring manual construction of a state-space map $h_S$.

The next two rows (\emph{Phase-inverted instruction table} and \emph{Parity-instrumented output register}, both at $N=3$) test whether boundary and dynamics-fragment verification remain invariant when subsystem teams refactor internal state representations or elaborate components with auxiliary diagnostic state. Both candidates modify a single component within the nominal $N=3$ coupling recipe $SCR_3$. The \emph{phase-inverted instruction table} design replaces $Z_3$ with an isomorphic component $Z_3'$ whose internal sense/act phase bit is inverted ($0\leftrightarrow 1$) together with its transition and readout tables, leaving $|S_{\mathrm{resultant}}|=512$ and $|S_{\mathrm{mode}}|=43$ unchanged. The \emph{parity-instrumented output register} design replaces $Z_2$ with an elaborated register $Z_2^+$ whose state space $S_2\times\{0,1\}$ appends an internal diagnostic parity bit that toggles on each processed input bit while emitting the same boundary pair $(\text{bit},\text{count})$; this doubles the resultant state space from $512$ to $1024$ states and expands the reachable operational mode from $43$ to $79$ states. Both designs pass all $22$ clauses of $\Phi_{IOR_3}$ without any change to the requirement specification. Whereas classical T3SD verification would require rewriting the state homomorphism $h_S$ to decode the inverted phase bit in $Z_3'$ or project away the diagnostic parity state in $Z_2^+$, Theorems~\ref{thm:encoding-invariance} and~\ref{thm:elaboration-coupling} ensure that temporal satisfaction absorbs internal re-encodings and state elaborations automatically, certifying that both refactored architectures remain in $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR_3,TYR_3)$.

The final four rows evaluate diagnostic rejection when a component in $TYR_3$ violates its behavioral contract or when the specification clock is misaligned with the hardware pipeline. The \emph{stuck sense/act head} design replaces $Z_1$ with a faulty head whose transition map fails to toggle and remains fixed in its initial phase; the checker rejects the resultant on the phase-alternation clause $\varphi_{\mathrm{mode}}$. The \emph{frozen input tape} design replaces $Z_0$ with a faulty tape whose transition map ignores incoming external bits and permanently retains its initial $0$ bit; because a $1$ bit is never latched, the reachable operational mode collapses from $43$ states to $9$ states and the checker rejects the design on the initial symbol-acquisition clause $\varphi_{\mathrm{start}}$. The \emph{non-resetting instruction table} design replaces $Z_3$ with a faulty table that continues incrementing the run count (up to $N=3$) when the input bit changes instead of resetting the count to $1$; although the resultant reaches $43$ mode states and satisfies the startup, hold, and same-bit increment clauses, the checker rejects it on the run-reset clause $\varphi_{\mathrm{reset}}$. Finally, the \emph{unshifted single-tick specification} candidate pairs the nominal, fault-free $N=3$ coupled resultant ($|S_{\mathrm{resultant}}|=512$, $|S_{\mathrm{mode}}|=43$) against an idealized single-tick requirement that expects the boundary output to update on every clock tick rather than holding across the two-phase sense/act cycle. The checker rejects this pairing on $\varphi_{\mathrm{hold}}$, confirming empirically the timing-alignment requirement of Proposition~\ref{prop:matched-ticks}: even when the underlying hardware components are sound, no lock-step Wymore homomorphism exists unless the specification and the buildable resultant share a matched clock granularity.
