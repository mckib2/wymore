\section{Case Study}
\label{sec:case-study}

This section walks through concrete reference systems and buildable implementations for a systems engineering audience. Each example has the same shape:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item State the required behavior in plain language and as a discrete-system table $Z_{\text{spec}}$.
    \item Compile the dynamics-encoding property set $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ from that table (Section~\ref{sec:dynamics-encoding-fragment}).
    \item Define three buildable systems $Z_{\text{impl}}$ of increasing internal complexity---with full next-state and readout definitions, not merely names---and show the projection maps into $Z_{\text{spec}}$.
    \item Check each buildable against the \emph{same} $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$; for the complex buildables, also verify the classical homomorphism by hand.
\end{enumerate}
Examples are adapted from Wymore's discrete-system illustrations \citep{Wymore1993}. For each complex buildable we also give an explicit classical homomorphism check (maps plus transition and readout preservation). On the finite recognizer the same maps admit exhibit/verify. The point of the simple/medium/complex ladders is \emph{maintenance across elaborations}: each ladder shares one compiled $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$, while the classical witness maps change with internal structure. The case studies are qualitative on that point---they exhibit the stable fragment beside evolving buildables; they do not measure verification effort.
