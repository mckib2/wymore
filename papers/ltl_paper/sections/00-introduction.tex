\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Systems engineering in recent years has transitioned toward Model-Based Systems Engineering (MBSE) to improve the precision, consistency, and verifiability of system specifications and architectural designs \citep{salado2019mbse,INCOSE2023}. Within the systems-theoretic literature, Wymore's Tricotyledon Theory of System Design (T3SD) provides a foundational mathematical framework for MBSE by formally separating what a system must do at its external boundary from how it can be constructed out of available components \citep{Wymore1993,salado2019wymore}. In T3SD, an input/output requirement ($IOR$) specifies the admissible input trajectories and their eligible output trajectories over an operational time scale, generating the \emph{functionality cotyledon} $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$, the space of all functional system designs whose boundary behavior satisfies $IOR$. Independently, a technology ($TYR$) cataloging available hardware and software components generates the \emph{buildability cotyledon} $\mathrm{CTL}(TYR)$, which comprises all coupled system designs $(Z_b,SCR)$ that can be assembled by selecting components from $TYR$ and wiring their ports according to a valid System Coupling Recipe ($SCR$) to form a coupled resultant $Z_b=\mathrm{RSY}(SCR)$.

Connecting these two design spaces is the central verification problem of T3SD. A candidate architecture belongs to the \emph{implementability cotyledon} $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR,TYR)$ when a buildable resultant $Z_b=\mathrm{RSY}(SCR)$ from $\mathrm{CTL}(TYR)$ \emph{implements} a functional system design $D_f=(Z_f,s_0,T)\in\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ \citep{Wymore1993}. Because a physical or software realization typically includes implementation artifacts that do not appear in an abstract functional specification (e.g., a built-in-test (BIT) and fault-monitoring states, sensor-polling and packet-framing cycles, redundancy warm-up modes, communication buffers, or bounded hardware capacities), T3SD defines implementation in two stages: the engineer first isolates an operational \emph{system mode} $Z_m\in\mathrm{SYSMO}(Z_b)$ capturing the relevant operating regime of the buildable resultant, and then exhibits a surjective, transition- and readout-preserving \emph{system homomorphism} $h=(h_S,h_I,h_O)$ from $Z_m$ onto $Z_f$.

While mathematically complete, carrying out this constructive verification workflow by hand presents practical difficulties. Whenever an $IOR$ is causally realizable, $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ contains infinitely many distinct state machines that produce the same eligible boundary trajectories \citep{Wymore1993}. Because implementable designs in $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR,TYR)$ are defined ass an explicit pair $(D_f,D_b)$ linked by a homomorphism onto a concrete functional system design $D_f=(Z_f,s_0,T)\in\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ (Definitions~5.71, 6.40, and~7.64 of \citet{Wymore1993}), classical T3SD verification does not check conformance against $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ directly; instead, the engineer must select a specific reference state machine $Z_{\mathrm{ref}}\in\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ and manually construct a surjective state-space mapping $h_S:S_m\twoheadrightarrow S_{\mathrm{ref}}$ from the coupled product state space of $Z_m$ onto $Z_{\mathrm{ref}}$. Deciding whether such a surjective homomorphism exists between finite transition systems is NP-hard in general \citep{HellNesetril1990,BodirskyKaraMartin2012}, and an explicit state map $h_S$ is fragile under iterative design revisions: changing a component's internal state encoding, permuting ports, or adding diagnostic state requires redefining and re-proving the homomorphism by hand. Moreover, T3SD has not developed the ecosystem of automated solvers and model checkers that is widely available for temporal logic \citep{Pnueli1977,BaierKatoen2008}.

In this paper, we address these limitations by establishing a formal correspondence between T3SD and temporal logic. Whereas classical T3SD is \emph{constructive}---specifying functionality by building explicit state machines and verifying conformance by exhibiting state-to-state homomorphisms---temporal logic is \emph{assertional}, specifying desired behavior through logical formulas over trajectories and testing whether a candidate system satisfies those formulas ($\models$) \citep{Pnueli1977,MannaPnueli1995}. First, we prove that every Wymorian $IOR$ translates into a characteristic boundary temporal specification $\Phi_{IOR}$ whose \emph{assertional functionality space} $\mathrm{AFSR}(\Phi_{IOR})$---the set of discrete systems satisfying the temporal assertions $\Phi_{IOR}$---is in canonical bijection with the functionality cotyledon $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$. Second, we show that this correspondence yields two complementary, automated verification workflows for any operational system mode $Z_m\in\mathrm{SYSMO}(Z_b)$:
(i) when the engineering requirement is given solely as a boundary $IOR$, checking that $Z_m$ satisfies $\Phi_{IOR}$ on its external ports ($Z_m\models_{IO}\Phi_{IOR}$) directly certifies membership in $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR,TYR)$ without requiring the engineer to invent an intermediate functional state machine or homomorphism; and
(ii) when a baseline functional design $D_{\mathrm{ref}}=(Z_{\mathrm{ref}},s_0,T)\in\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ has been selected---as classical constructive T3SD requires for an implementable pair in $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR,TYR)$ (Definitions~5.71, 6.40, and~7.64 of \citet{Wymore1993})---the transition and readout tables of $Z_{\mathrm{ref}}$ can be automatically compiled into step-local temporal safety rules $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}(Z_{\mathrm{ref}})$ whose satisfaction ($Z_m\models_{\mathrm{dyn}}\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}(Z_{\mathrm{ref}})$) is equivalent to the existence of a surjective Wymore homomorphism $h:Z_m\twoheadrightarrow Z_{\mathrm{ref}}$. Because physical digital and embedded software components in a technology catalog $TYR$ are typically finite-state machines \citep{Wymore1993,BurchClarkeMcMillanDill1992,BaierKatoen2008}, any coupled resultant $Z_b=\mathrm{RSY}(SCR)$ and any operational mode $Z_m\in\mathrm{SYSMO}(Z_b)$ is a finite transition system; consequently, when $\Phi_{IOR}$ is expressed in Linear Temporal Logic (LTL), verifying $Z_m\models_{IO}\Phi_{IOR}$ is decidable, automated, and invariant across internal state re-encodings and componentwise elaborations. We illustrate the approach through a case study on a four-component coupled streaming Run-Length Encoder (RLE), tracing its refinement from an idealized infinite-tape requirement $IOR_\infty$ to a verified family of finite-tape buildable machines $Z_{b,N}=\mathrm{RSY}(SCR_N)$.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:background} summarizes the notation correspondence with \citet{Wymore1993}, introduces discrete systems, the three cotyledons, system modes, homomorphisms, and temporal logic in systems engineering terms, and reviews related literature. Section~\ref{sec:methodology} develops the boundary $IOR$ isomorphism, the direct mode-to-$IOR$ and reference-dynamics verification theorems, and the invariance and compositional propagation properties. Section~\ref{sec:case-study} presents the streaming RLE case study from its infinite-tape formulation through its finite-tape refinement, synchronous pipeline timing, and automated LTL verification. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses implications for T3SD practice, the complementary roles of $\Phi_{IOR}$ and $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$, and limitations of the approach. Section~\ref{sec:conclusions} concludes with directions for future research.
