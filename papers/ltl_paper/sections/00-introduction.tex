\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Systems engineering continues its transition toward Model-Based Systems Engineering (MBSE) to improve the precision, consistency, and verifiability of system specifications and architectural designs \citep{salado2019mbse,INCOSE2023}. Within the systems-theoretic literature, Wymore's Tricotyledon Theory of System Design (T3SD) provides a foundational mathematical framework for MBSE by formally separating what a system must do at its external boundary from how it can be constructed out of available components \citep{Wymore1993,salado2019wymore}. In T3SD, an Input/Output Requirement ($IOR$) specifies the admissible input trajectories and their eligible output trajectories over an operational horizon, generating the \emph{functionality cotyledon} $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$, the space of all functional system designs whose boundary behavior satisfies $IOR$. Independently, a technology ($TYR$) cataloging available hardware and/or software components together with a System Coupling Recipe ($SCR$) wiring their ports generate the \emph{buildability cotyledon} $\mathrm{CTL}(TYR)$, whose members are coupled resultant systems $Z_b=\mathrm{RSY}(SCR)$.

Connecting these two design spaces is the central verification problem of T3SD. A candidate architecture belongs to the \emph{implementability cotyledon} $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR,TYR)$ when a buildable resultant $Z_b\in\mathrm{CTL}(TYR)$ \emph{implements} a functional system design $D_f=(Z_f,s_0,T)\in\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ \citep{Wymore1993}. Because a physical instantiation of a system typically contains implementation artifacts that are absent from an abstract functional model (such as multi-phase internal clocks, initialization states, diagnostic registers, or bounded hardware capacity), Wymore defines implementation in two stages: the engineer first isolates an operational \emph{system mode} $Z_m\in\mathrm{SYSMO}(Z_b)$ capturing the relevant operating regime of the buildable resultant, and then exhibits a surjective, transition- and readout-preserving \emph{system homomorphism} $h=(h_S,h_I,h_O)$ from $Z_m$ onto $Z_f$.

While mathematically complete, carrying out this constructive verification workflow by hand exhibits important practical limitations. Whenever an $IOR$ is causally realizable, $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ contains infinitely many state-space realizations that produce the same eligible boundary trajectories \citep{Wymore1993}. In classical T3SD, the engineer typically does not check conformance against $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ in the abstract; instead, one must select a particular reference state machine $Z_{\mathrm{ref}}\in\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ and manually construct a surjective state-space mapping $h_S:S_m\twoheadrightarrow S_{\mathrm{ref}}$ from the coupled product state space of $Z_m$ onto $Z_{\mathrm{ref}}$. Deciding whether such a surjective homomorphism exists between finite transition systems is NP-hard in general \citep{HellNesetril1990,BodirskyKaraMartin2012}, and the explicit mapping $h_S$ is fragile under iterative design changes: modifying a component's internal state encoding, permuting ports, or even adding a diagnostic counter requires reconstructing and re-proving the homomorphism by hand. Moreover, because T3SD has remained a notoriously esoteric systems engineering theory (deservedly or not), it has not developed the ecosystem of automated solvers and model checkers that is commonplace for temporal logic \citep{Pnueli1977,BaierKatoen2008}.

In this paper, we address these limitations by establishing a formal correspondance between T3SD and temporal logic. We show that temporal logic provides an exact, tool-supported language for characterizing the functionality cotyledon and automatically certifying the implementability of buildable system modes. First, we prove that every Wymorian $IOR$ translates into a characteristic boundary temporal specification $\Phi_{IOR}$ whose assertional functionality space $\mathrm{AFSR}(\Phi_{IOR})$ is in canonical bijection with $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$. Second, using Wymore's requirement-preservation theorem alongside a compiled reference-dynamics safety fragment $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}(Z_{\mathrm{ref}})$, we prove that a system mode $Z_m\in\mathrm{SYSMO}(Z_b)$ admits an identity-boundary homomorphism onto a functional design in $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ if and only if $Z_m$ satisfies $\Phi_{IOR}$ directly ($Z_m\models_{IO}\Phi_{IOR}$), and admits a homomorphism onto a specific reference machine $Z_{\mathrm{ref}}$ if and only if $Z_m\models_{\mathrm{dyn}}\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}(Z_{\mathrm{ref}})$. Because real-world components in a technology catalog $TYR$ are typically finite-state machines, any coupled resultant $Z_b=\mathrm{RSY}(SCR)$ and any operational mode $Z_m\in\mathrm{SYSMO}(Z_b)$ is a finite transition system; consequently, when $\Phi_{IOR}$ is expressed in Linear Temporal Logic (LTL), verifying $Z_m\models_{IO}\Phi_{IOR}$ is decidable, automated, and invariant across internal state re-encodings and componentwise elaborations. We illustrate the approach through a case study on a four-component coupled streaming Run-Length Encoder (RLE), tracing its refinement from an idealized infinite-tape requirement $IOR_\infty$ to a verified family of finite-tape buildable machines $Z_{b,N}=\mathrm{RSY}(SCR_N)$.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:background} reviews discrete systems, the three cotyledons, system modes, homomorphisms, and temporal logic in systems engineering terms, and reviews related literature. Section~\ref{sec:methodology} develops the boundary $IOR$ isomorphism, the direct mode-to-$IOR$ and reference-dynamics verification theorems, and the invariance and compositional lifting properties. Section~\ref{sec:case-study} presents the streaming RLE case study from its infinite-tape formulation through its finite-tape refinement, pipeline timing analysis, and automated LTL verification. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses implications for T3SD practice, the complementary roles of $\Phi_{IOR}$ and $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$, and limitations of the approach. Section~\ref{sec:conclusions} concludes with directions for future research.
