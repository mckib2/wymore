\section{Conclusions}

We propose an assertional reinterpretation of Wymore's Functionality Cotyledon in which functional intent is expressed as a dynamics-encoding temporal fragment compiled from a discrete system. On that fragment, assertional satisfaction is equivalent to classical implementability via Wymore system homomorphism, so modelers may treat property checking of the compiled fragment as the primary verification artifact rather than re-deriving an explicit homomorphism through successive lifecycle iterations of the system-as-built. Case studies on a ones-counter, a string recognizer (including a shift-register refactor), and a real-valued accumulator exhibit that maintenance pattern qualitatively, with finite-tier exhibit/verify of maps where applicable. Checking decidability requires finite projections or bounded queries; global equivalence for properties outside the fragment, and automatic map discovery on infinite or real state, remain as future work.
