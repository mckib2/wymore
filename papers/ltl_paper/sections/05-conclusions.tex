\section{Conclusions}

We propose an assertional reinterpretation of Wymore's Functionality Cotyledon in which functional intent is expressed as a dynamics-encoding temporal fragment compiled from a discrete system. On that fragment, assertional satisfaction is equivalent to classical implementability via Wymore system homomorphism, so modelers may treat property checking of the compiled fragment as the primary verification artifact rather than re-deriving an explicit homomorphism through successive iterations of the system-as-built. Case studies on a Nat incrementing counter, saturating subtracter, clear/add accumulator, and zero-test---including a binary-shift refactor of the counter---and a Fibonacci composition assembled from those shelf zones exhibit that maintenance pattern qualitatively---including the T3SD theme of assembling mismatched available parts under an honest technology bound---with exhibit/verify of maps where applicable. Checking decidability requires finite projections or bounded queries; global equivalence for properties outside the fragment, automatic map discovery on infinite state, and instrumented CI remain as future work.
