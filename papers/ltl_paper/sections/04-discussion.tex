\section{Discussion} \label{sec:discussion}

Encoding functional intent as temporal properties moves requirements from informal statements into a checkable language aligned with open-system boundary practice \citep{salado2019mbse,BaierKatoen2008}. The assertional reading developed here is a reinterpretation of the Functionality Cotyledon for verification workflow, not a replacement of T3SD when a reference system and homomorphism already exist and remain convenient.

\subsection{Relationship to Classical Functionality Cotyledon}
\label{sec:classical-fc}

On the dynamics-encoding bridge fragment, for fixed $Z_{\text{spec}}$,
\[
  Z_{\text{impl}} \in \mathrm{FC}_{\text{constructive}}(Z_{\text{spec}})
  \;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;
  Z_{\text{impl}} \models_{\mathrm{dyn}} \Phi_{Z_{\text{spec}}},
\]
where $\mathrm{FC}_{\text{constructive}}(Z_{\text{spec}})$ is the set of buildable systems that admit a Wymore system homomorphism into $Z_{\text{spec}}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:hom-biimp}; Section~\ref{sec:case-study}). The property sets exhibited for the incrementing counter, saturating subtracter, Nat accumulator, and zero-test are instances of Table~\ref{tab:phi-dyn-fragment}.

Formulas outside the dynamics-encoding fragment do not, in general, imply existence of such an $h$. As a concrete contrast: $F(\mathit{output}(1))$ may hold of a buildable system that eventually pulses a bit without agreeing with a given reference transition table, and a bare input/output safety property may be shared by systems with different next-state functions. Those cases lie outside the bi-implication; the bridge requires a complete $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ (compiled from $Z_{\text{spec}}$, or authored in the same schemas).

Global set equality between constructive FC and assertional FC for arbitrary $\Phi$ outside the fragment remains open. Output-table-only encodings and $F$ formulas conflated with dynamics tables are outside the bi-implication.

\subsection{Expressivity, Checking, and Decidability}
\label{sec:expressivity}
\label{sec:limitations}

Restricting to $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ buys the homomorphism bi-implication; it also limits what engineers can \emph{say}. The fragment admits only table-shaped $G$/$X$ clauses compiled from a reference $\delta$ and $\rho$ (Table~\ref{tab:phi-dyn-fragment}): no $F$, no $U$, and no free-form quantification over values beyond indexing those clauses. Richer dialects---propositional LTL with the full operator set, or FO-LTL over infinite coordinates---let engineers author desired behavior beyond that fragment, at the cost of leaving the strict T3SD implementability bridge.

Two concrete contrasts from the case studies make the gap vivid. For the zero-test, $\Phi_{Z_{\mathrm{jz}}}$ requires every guided sample of the reference latch. In FO-LTL one can instead write
\[
  F\bigl(\mathit{output}(1)\bigr)
\]
(``eventually report zero''). That formula is easy to author as a boundary requirement, but it does not constrain \emph{which} samples produced the flag: a buildable that reports zero for the wrong reason still satisfies it, and satisfaction is not equivalent to a Wymore homomorphism into $Z_{\mathrm{jz}}$. For the Fibonacci composition, FO-LTL can state a liveness that $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ cannot express at all without changing the reference system itself:
\[
  F\bigl(\mathit{phase}(\mathit{done})\bigr)
\]
(``eventually halt''). The dynamics-encoding fragment has no $F$ and only next-state/readout schemas; ``eventually done'' is not a row of any fixed transition table. Response patterns such as $G\bigl(\mathit{load}(n)\rightarrow F(\mathit{done})\bigr)$ likewise lie outside $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$.

The engineering tradeoff is therefore deliberate. Use $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ when the question is classical implementability of a stated $Z_{\text{spec}}$. Use a more expressive fragment when the question is whether a buildable meets authored boundary intent---accepting that the answer no longer coincides with existence of a homomorphism.

The bi-implication itself is a \emph{semantic} equivalence. It does not imply that checking $Z_{\text{impl}} \models_{\mathrm{dyn}} \Phi_{Z_{\text{spec}}}$ is decidable for arbitrary systems. On finite state, input, and output spaces, propositional encodings of the fragment admit standard finite-state decision procedures. For infinite discrete state, FO satisfaction is not decidable in general \citep{BaierKatoen2008}; practical constraints include finite abstraction, bounded-horizon unrolling, restricted input classes, and theory-specific queries. Automatic discovery of homomorphism maps on infinite state is not claimed; on the kit zones and Fibonacci composition, maps are exhibited and verified (Sections~\ref{sec:case-shift-counter}--\ref{sec:case-fib}).

Relative to classical hand-maintained homomorphisms, the case studies illustrate maintenance across evolving buildables: a single compiled $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ is re-checked against direct and alternate encodings, while the classical approach requires a structure-specific witness at each iteration. The binary-shift counter is the structural-divergence exhibit: $h_S$ is no longer a component projection. The Fibonacci composition (Section~\ref{sec:case-fib}) sharpens the same contrast under wiring: assembling $F_n$ from shelf increment, decrement, accumulate, and zero-test zones forces classical rebuild of the coupled witness, whereas the assertional side re-checks the compiled Fibonacci fragment. That is a workflow contrast under the bi-implication, not a claim that map discovery is automatic or that property checking is always cheaper. The evidence is qualitative, not a timed benchmark.

Because compiled $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ fragments are stable artifacts indexed by $Z_{\text{spec}}$ (or by a composed table), they are natural candidates for regression checks in a continuous-integration pipeline when a buildable zone changes: re-run property checking of the affected fragment against the revised transition and readout tables. Classical product or cascade homomorphisms do not embed as cleanly as such checks---each part swap tends to demand a fresh human derivation of $h$. We do \emph{not} claim to have instrumented CI or measured wall-clock savings in this paper; the point is an engineering fit suggested by the assertional workflow.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Open:} global FC equality for arbitrary $\Phi$ outside the fragment.
    \item \textbf{Not claimed:} automatic homomorphism search on infinite state; continuous-time hybrid verification; instrumented CI or timed verification-effort benchmarks.
    \item \textbf{Future work:} SysML tooling integration; CI-embedded $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ checking for finite zones; broader empirical comparison of verification effort; deeper related-work engagement with runtime verification and supervisory control.
\end{itemize}
