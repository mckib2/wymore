\section{Discussion} \label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Implications for T3SD Verification}
\label{sec:implications}

Wymore's Tricotyledon Theory of System Design provides a mathematically rigorous foundation for systems engineering by separating what a system must do at its external boundary ($\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$) from how it is constructed out of available components ($\mathrm{CTL}(TYR)$), and by defining implementability ($\mathrm{CTL}(IOR,TYR)$) through homomorphisms from operational system modes $Z_m\in\mathrm{SYSMO}(Z_b)$ onto functional designs $Z_f\in\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$. Despite this conceptual clarity, T3SD has seen limited practical adoption for formal verification. This lack of common use contributes to the absence of automated tooling support: whereas the formal methods community developed mature explicit-state, symbolic BDD, and SAT/SMT model checkers for temporal logic over four decades \citep{Pnueli1977,MannaPnueli1995,BiereCimattiClarkeZhu1999,BaierKatoen2008}, applying T3SD constructively required selecting a reference state machine $Z_{\mathrm{ref}}\in\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ and maintaining an explicit state-space homomorphism $h=(h_S,h_I,h_O)$ by hand. As coupled architectures grow in state count or undergo iterative design revisions, manual homomorphism construction and maintenance becomes impractical.

The correspondence established in this paper allows systems engineers to retain Wymore's tricotyledon structure while leveraging existing temporal logic verification tools. Because Theorem~\ref{thm:boundary-characteristic} proves that every $IOR$ is canonically isomorphic to a boundary temporal specification $\Phi_{IOR}$, expressing the eligibility relation $ER(f)$ in temporal logic preserves T3SD's trajectory semantics while making Moore causality and pipeline latency constraints explicit before any hardware architecture is selected. Furthermore, while idealized functional specifications ($IOR_\infty$) may involve infinite state spaces, physical or digital components in a buildable technology catalog $TYR$ are typically finite-state. Consequently, every coupled resultant $Z_b=\mathrm{RSY}(SCR)\in\mathrm{CTL}(TYR)$ and every operational mode $Z_m\in\mathrm{SYSMO}(Z_b)$ is a finite Kripke structure, reducing implementability certification to decidable LTL model checking (Theorems~\ref{thm:mode-ior-bridge} and~\ref{thm:dynamics-characterization}).

The invariance and canonicity results of Section~\ref{sec:invariance} further support iterative systems engineering workflows. As illustrated by the phase-inverted instruction table and parity-instrumented output register designs in Table~\ref{tab:solver-results}, when a subsystem team re-encodes a component's internal state, relabels ports, or elaborates a component with diagnostic counters, the temporal specifications $\Phi_{IOR}$ and $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ remain unchanged (Theorems~\ref{thm:encoding-invariance} and~\ref{thm:elaboration-coupling}). In addition, when engineers compare alternative finite-state reference models or candidate subsystem architectures during trade studies, Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-canonicity} shows that mutual satisfaction of compiled dynamics fragments ($Z_1\models_{\mathrm{dyn}}\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}(Z_2)$ and $Z_2\models_{\mathrm{dyn}}\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}(Z_1)$) yields a Wymore system isomorphism because $\models_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ includes surjective Definition~4.3 witnesses \citep{Wymore1993}.

\subsection{Roles of \texorpdfstring{$\Phi_{IOR}$ and $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$}{Phi\_IOR and Phi\_dyn}}
\label{sec:complementary-roles}

The proposed methodology provides two distinct temporal verification targets corresponding to different stages of the system design lifecycle. Direct boundary verification ($Z_m\models_{IO}\Phi_{IOR}$) applies when the engineering requirement is specified purely in terms of admissible input and output trajectories (such as the boundary specification in Table~\ref{tab:rle-ltl-clauses} for the finite-tape RLE); in this case, Theorem~\ref{thm:mode-ior-bridge} certifies membership in $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR,TYR)$ directly from the boundary behavior of the mode $Z_m$, without requiring the engineer to design an intermediate reference state machine $Z_{\mathrm{ref}}$. Conversely, reference-dynamics verification ($Z_m\models_{\mathrm{dyn}}\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}(Z_{\mathrm{ref}})$) applies when a baseline functional architecture $D_{\mathrm{ref}}=(Z_{\mathrm{ref}},s_0,T)\in\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$ has already been established and subsystem teams are elaborating its components; Theorem~\ref{thm:dynamics-characterization} and Theorem~\ref{thm:elaboration-coupling} then verify that the detailed mode $Z_m$ is a valid homomorphic elaboration of $Z_{\mathrm{ref}}$.

Separating these two checks also sharpens failure diagnosis during integration. If a candidate mode $Z_m$ fails $\Phi_{IOR}$, it violates the boundary requirement outright and cannot implement any functional design in $\mathrm{CTL}(IOR)$. If $Z_m$ satisfies $\Phi_{IOR}$ on its external boundary ports but fails $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}(Z_{\mathrm{ref}})$, then $Z_m$ is a valid functional realization of $IOR$ that achieves the required boundary behavior through a different internal state organization or timing decomposition than the chosen reference machine $Z_{\mathrm{ref}}$.

\subsection{Limitations and Scope}
\label{sec:limitations}

This work carries with it several limitations. First, because a classical Wymore homomorphism is a lock-step map equating one tick of the buildable mode $Z_m$ with one tick of the functional design $Z_f$, staged implementations with multi-tick sense/act cycles or internal Moore pipeline registers must match the clock granularity of the specification (as discussed in Section~\ref{sec:case-machine}). An unshifted specification that ignores pipeline latency (such as the unshifted single-tick specification in Table~\ref{tab:solver-results}) will be rejected even when the underlying state-update logic is otherwise sound. Second, automated propositional LTL model checking depends on the finiteness of the operational system mode $Z_m$; while buildable digital components in $TYR$ are finite-state, idealized requirements over unbounded state spaces (such as $IOR_\infty$) or continuous physical observables must first be refined or discretized into finite operational regimes before finite-state model checking can be applied. Third, although explicit-state reachability and LTL checking can easily discharge our four-component RLE modes on consumer-grade hardware, composing a large number of concurrent components in a coupling recipe $SCR$ remains subject to Cartesian product state-space growth across $\prod_{i=1}^n S_i$.
