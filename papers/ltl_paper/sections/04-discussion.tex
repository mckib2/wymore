\section{Discussion} \label{sec:discussion}

The two correspondences answer different questions and carry different checking
obligations.

\subsection{Expressivity and lock-step}
\label{sec:limitations}

The characteristic boundary construction covers every Wymorian IOR by retaining
its time set, admissible trajectories, and eligible-output relation as semantic
objects. A finite ordinary-LTL presentation requires additional structure.
Echo, delay, and run length have compact presentations; arbitrary trajectory
relations may require symbolic predicates or direct semantic reasoning.

$\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ uses $G$/$X$ clauses for readout and one-step dynamics.
The RLE requirement states the run so far. Eventual production of an encoding
for an entire infinite input is a separate liveness property, outside the
homomorphism bi-implication.

Conformance is lock-step. Section~\ref{sec:case-ticks} shows that a two-phase
build fails a one-tick reference and satisfies the behavior restated at two
ticks. Rate-tolerant conformance would require a different implementation
relation.

The scope is therefore precise: the homomorphism result applies to the
step-local schemas of $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ and to matching clock rates. Other
temporal properties may be checked alongside the fragment, with guarantees
supplied by their own semantics.

\subsection{Implications for systems engineering}
\label{sec:implications}

The fragments change which artifacts are maintained, not which designs are
admissible. $\Phi_R$ is a presentation of the IOR: it can be inspected,
specialized to compact temporal laws when the boundary relation permits, and
used as the criterion that generates $\mathrm{FSR}(R)$. $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$
is a presentation of a chosen functional design's dynamics. Implementation
work then consists in checking a buildable resultant against that fragment,
rather than reconstructing pair-specific maps at each revision.

That split matches how engineers already separate requirement satisfaction from
realization of a selected functional design. When a candidate fails
$\Phi_R$, it is not a functional design for $R$. When it satisfies $\Phi_R$
but fails $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$, it may still be functional for $R$ while
failing to realise the chosen $D_{\mathrm{ref}}$. Shared-table specializations
make the second failure a table disagreement. Hom-relative checking covers the
case in which state or interface encodings differ.

Coupling latency belongs in the IOR when the architecture is staged. The
run-length case is the concrete instance: a one-tick encoder is a legitimate
functional design, but it is the wrong implementation reference for a
four-component synchronous resultant. Recording pipeline delay at the boundary
keeps the implementability pairing honest and prevents a failed check from
being misread as a coding error.

The reuse theorems give a path to larger architectures without changing
Wymore's implementation relation. A component can be checked against a
fragment compiled from its own reference; a coupling witness then lifts those
results to the resultant; later re-encodings keep the same fragment as the
target. Finite components become generated decision problems. Infinite
components remain symbolic, but they need not be re-proved from scratch when
only a neighboring component changes. The search for maps is still hard in
general. Decomposition changes the size of each obligation, not the underlying
decision problem.

Buildability remains a separate argument. Fragment satisfaction does not
populate a technology or invent a coupling recipe. The implementability
cotyledon still pairs a functional design with a buildable design; the
assertional check supplies the implementation witness that Wymore requires of
that pair.

\subsection{Tooling}
\label{sec:tooling}

Finite systems admit a propositional check, and bounded counts or horizons
provide finite projections of infinite systems. Symbolic arguments cover the
unbounded RLE result. T3SD coupling supports lifting component results when the
coupling witness is supplied. The present case proves the four-component
resultant directly and demonstrates component-level reuse on the instruction
table.

\subsection{Future work}
\label{sec:future}

Several extensions sit outside the present correspondences and are worth
stating as such.

A $k$-tick or stuttering implementation relation would address staged
architectures without restating the reference clock. That relation is not
Wymore's homomorphism, so the dynamics fragment and its bi-implication would
need to be rebuilt around it.

Liveness and progress can be attached as companion properties. The RLE
encoder's eventual emission of a complete encoding, fairness of a scheduler,
or eventual emptying of a buffer are natural examples. Their guarantees would
come from their own semantics, not from Theorem~\ref{thm:dynamics-characterization}.

Compilation and checking can be made more mechanical: generate $\Phi_R$ from
an IOR presentation, generate $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ from a discrete-system
table, and discharge finite instances with existing SAT, SMT, or model-checking
tools. The RLE projections show the encoding; they are not a library or a
measured workflow.

Hierarchical case studies would exercise Theorem~\ref{thm:elaboration-coupling}
at more than one coupling depth, including mixed finite and infinite
components. Industrial examples and effort measurements against constructive
homomorphism proofs would test whether the change in maintained artifact
reduces engineering labor. Performance and other Wymore requirement types
remain outside $\Phi_R$ and $\Phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ as formulated here.
